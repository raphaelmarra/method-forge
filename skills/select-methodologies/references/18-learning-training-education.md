# Learning, training, education, and instructional systems

Use this catalog when the intended outcome is learning, skill transfer, behavior change, curriculum design, assessment, or training-system evaluation. Do not confuse content production, instructional design, pedagogy, assessment, and program evaluation.

## Needs and performance analysis

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Performance gap analysis | diagnostic method; desired vs actual performance, causes, and candidate interventions | a business/mission outcome is poor and training is only one possible remedy | assume every gap is lack of knowledge rather than incentives, tools, process, authority, workload, or design |
| Training Needs Analysis — TNA | needs-assessment process at organization, task, and person levels | determine who needs what capability and under which work conditions | begin from a requested course title without validating the performance need |
| Job / Cognitive Task Analysis | elicitation and modeling family; tasks, decisions, cues, knowledge, errors, and conditions | expert performance or safety-critical judgment must become teachable and assessable | decompose adaptive expertise into rote steps or rely on expert self-report alone |
| Competency modeling | representation method; observable capabilities, proficiency levels, and evidence | roles, development paths, and assessments need a shared capability model | create vague traits, overlapping competencies, or levels without observable evidence |
| Learner and context analysis | discovery method; prior knowledge, goals, access, language, constraints, motivation, and environment | design decisions depend on heterogeneous learners and delivery conditions | use demographic stereotypes or collect personal data with no instructional decision attached |

## Instructional and curriculum design

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ADDIE | generic instructional-systems lifecycle; analyze, design, develop, implement, evaluate | a complete training product needs traceable phases and evaluation | treat phases as one-pass waterfall or cite the acronym as a detailed method by itself |
| Backward Design | curriculum-design method; desired results → acceptable evidence → learning experiences | assessments and activities must align to durable outcomes | objectives are unknown/exploratory or fixed content is merely relabeled after development |
| Constructive Alignment | curriculum principle/framework | intended outcomes, learning activities, and assessment should require the same kind of cognition/performance | alignment narrows the curriculum to easily tested behaviors or ignores emergent learning |
| Design Thinking / co-design for learning | participatory design approach | learner experience, access barriers, and context are uncertain and prototypes can be tested | ideation substitutes for learning science, subject expertise, or validity evidence |
| Successive Approximation Model — SAM | iterative/proprietary instructional-design approach | rapid prototypes and stakeholder reviews can reduce design uncertainty | compliance-critical instruction needs baselined traceability but iteration is undocumented |
| Universal Design for Learning — UDL | accessibility/inclusion framework | design flexible means of engagement, representation, and action/expression around a common goal | reduce rigor, personalize without constraints, or assume UDL alone satisfies legal accessibility requirements |
| Bloom's revised taxonomy | objective-classification heuristic | vary and inspect cognitive demand in goals and assessment | treat levels as a mandatory staircase, complete pedagogy, or proof of task complexity |
| Learning objective + evidence specification | contract artifact | each capability needs conditions, observable performance, criterion, and acceptable evidence | use verbs such as “understand” without evidence or teach only what is easiest to count |
| Curriculum map / prerequisite graph | representation artifact | concepts, capabilities, sequence, dependencies, and coverage need visibility | assume one linear path fits all learners or confuse exposure with mastery |

## Learning and practice techniques

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explicit instruction + worked examples | teaching technique | novices need accurate models, guided practice, and gradual removal of support | advanced learners require open diagnosis/transfer or examples encourage copying without self-explanation |
| Cognitive apprenticeship | instructional framework; modeling, coaching, scaffolding, articulation, reflection, and exploration | tacit expert reasoning in authentic tasks must become visible | access to representative practice and competent coaching is absent |
| Retrieval practice | learning technique | durable recall and discrimination benefit from effortful low-stakes recall with feedback | quizzes become punitive grading, only isolated facts are retrieved, or feedback is absent |
| Spaced practice | scheduling technique | learning must remain available over time | one urgent just-in-time procedure is the only goal or spacing prevents prerequisite fluency |
| Interleaving | practice-scheduling technique | learners must discriminate among related problem types or strategies | novices lack basic schemas or random mixing destroys coherent initial explanation |
| Mastery learning | instructional approach; clear objectives, formative checks, corrective support, and additional time | prerequisite knowledge accumulates and variable time/support can be provided | “mastery” is a single arbitrary test score or pacing/resources cannot support correction and reassessment |
| Deliberate practice | structured practice technique | performance has valid tasks, immediate informative feedback, repeated correction, and expert mental models | routine repetition is mislabeled deliberate practice or feedback/standards are weak |
| Problem-/case-based learning | instructional approach | integrated reasoning, ambiguity, and transfer through authentic problems are objectives | novices receive minimal guidance or factual/prerequisite gaps block useful inquiry |
| Scenario, simulation, and serious-game learning | experiential method | rare, costly, hazardous, interpersonal, or dynamic situations can be practiced safely | simulation fidelity is impressive but decision cues, feedback, transfer, and debrief are invalid |
| Team-Based Learning | structured collaborative method | preparation, readiness checks, and consequential team application fit a stable cohort | group work lacks individual accountability or tasks do not require team reasoning |
| Reflective practice / after-action review | learning method | experience should be converted into explicit observations, explanations, and next actions | reflection becomes unstructured opinion or replaces performance evidence |
| Just-in-time performance support | workplace intervention; checklists, prompts, decision aids, and searchable guidance | infrequent or changing knowledge is better supported at task time than memorized | task requires fluent automatic performance under conditions where aids are unavailable |

## Assessment and measurement

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assessment blueprint / table of specifications | representation and assurance artifact | content, cognitive demand, weighting, and item/task coverage must trace to objectives | one blueprint freezes an evolving domain or guarantees item validity by itself |
| Formative assessment | learning-assessment process | evidence during learning can trigger feedback, reteaching, practice, or learner adjustment | collect frequent scores without an actionable response |
| Summative assessment | decision assessment | certify attainment at a defined point under appropriate reliability and validity controls | use one high-stakes measure beyond its validated interpretation |
| Performance-based assessment | authentic assessment method | capability is best demonstrated through doing, producing, diagnosing, or deciding | scoring criteria, task comparability, safety, and rater consistency are uncontrolled |
| Rubric / behaviorally anchored rating scale | scoring representation | complex performance needs transparent dimensions and levels | descriptors are vague, dimensions overlap, or numerical totals imply unjustified interval precision |
| Item analysis / Classical Test Theory — CTT | psychometric technique | difficulty, discrimination, reliability, and test-score quality need evaluation for a defined population | item properties are assumed invariant across populations or reliability is mistaken for validity |
| Item Response Theory — IRT | psychometric model family | item/person parameters, adaptive testing, linking, or scale precision justify model complexity and sample size | dimensionality, local independence, fit, fairness, or sufficient data cannot be established |
| Standard setting | judgment/evidence method; defensible performance cut scores | consequential pass/fail or proficiency decisions require documented expert procedure | a convenient percentage is labeled “mastery” without task and consequence evidence |
| Assessment validity argument | structured assurance case | justify score interpretation and use through claims, evidence, assumptions, and consequences | report one coefficient as proof that every use of a test is valid |
| Differential Item Functioning / fairness review | assurance technique | comparable groups may experience items or scoring differently after controlling for ability | statistical flags alone declare bias or small samples hide material inequity |

## Training and program evaluation

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kirkpatrick Four Levels | training-evaluation framework; reaction, learning, behavior, and results | plan evidence beyond attendance/satisfaction and inspect workplace transfer/outcomes | treat levels as a guaranteed causal chain or attribute business results to training without a design |
| CIPP — Context, Input, Process, Product | program-evaluation framework | improvement and accountability require evidence across need, design, implementation, and outcomes | a simple course needs only focused learning/transfer measures |
| Logic model / Theory of Change | program representation | inputs, activities, mechanisms, outputs, outcomes, assumptions, and indicators need explicit linkage | use the diagram as proof of causality; see `13-legal-policy-ethics.md` and `08-decision-optimization-causality.md` |
| Transfer evaluation | evaluation method | determine whether capability is applied at work and which opportunity/support conditions enable it | infer transfer from learner intention or manager satisfaction alone |
| Training ROI / cost-effectiveness | economic evaluation technique | decision makers need transparent cost vs monetized benefit or outcome-unit comparison | monetize weakly identified outcomes, omit opportunity cost, or present proprietary multipliers as facts |
| Experimental / quasi-experimental impact evaluation | causal design family | the effect of training or education policy must be separated from selection, maturation, and trends | randomization is unethical/infeasible without a credible alternative; see causal catalog |
| Learning analytics | measurement/diagnostic family | digital traces can identify engagement, progression, struggle, and intervention opportunities | clicks/time are treated as learning, surveillance lacks governance, or models create self-fulfilling labels |

## Learning technology representations

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SCORM | packaging/runtime interoperability standard | legacy LMS course launch, sequencing, and completion exchange require compatibility | capture rich cross-system experience or use compliance as proof of instructional quality |
| Experience API — xAPI | event statement specification | learning and performance events across tools/environments need interoperable capture | verb/object freedom creates incomparable data without a governed profile and identity model |
| cmi5 | xAPI profile for LMS-launched content | combine LMS launch/session rules with xAPI statements | a full curriculum, pedagogy, or analytics method is expected from the protocol |
| Learning Tools Interoperability — LTI | integration standard | external learning tools need standardized LMS launch, identity/role, and grade exchange | authorization, privacy, pedagogy, or content quality are assumed from interoperability |
| Competency and credential schemas | representation standards | capabilities, evidence, credentials, and mappings must move between systems | schema alignment substitutes for verifying issuer, assessment validity, or current competence |

## Composition patterns

### Build workplace training for a new operational process

`performance-gap analysis → task/cognitive task analysis → competency and objective contracts → backward design → worked examples + guided authentic practice → formative assessment → performance assessment → job aid → transfer supports → Kirkpatrick-informed evaluation + causal design if attribution matters`

### Teach an agent or person to execute a high-risk workflow

`task/state analysis → critical errors and authority boundaries → demonstration → simulation with fault scenarios → mastery gate on representative performance → supervised practice → independent verification → refresher/decay monitoring → incident-driven curriculum update`

### Evaluate whether training worked

`decision and estimand → baseline + comparison strategy → exposure/fidelity → learning measure validity → workplace opportunity and behavior evidence → operational outcome + guardrails → causal analysis/sensitivity → cost-effectiveness → decision record`

Satisfaction is not learning; learning is not transfer; transfer is not proof of business impact. Keep the constructs and evidence chains separate.

## Research anchors and status

Status checked 2026-08-12.

- [CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/) is the current 2024 version of the UDL guidance.
- [UNESCO learning-assessment resources](https://www.unesco.org/en/learning-assessments) and its principles of good practice cover assessment purpose, quality, equity, implementation, and use.
- [Education Endowment Foundation Teaching and Learning Toolkit](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit) is a living evidence-synthesis gateway for instructional approaches; check the technical appendix and context before importing effect summaries.
- [Kirkpatrick's official current model](https://www.kirkpatrickpartners.com/the-kirkpatrick-model/) remains proprietary/owner-promoted. Use the four constructs as an evaluation scaffold, not independent causal evidence.
- ADDIE has no single current normative owner/version. Label it an established generic lifecycle, not a certifiable standard.
- For assessment claims, seek standards or guidance from the relevant education authority and professional testing bodies, then validate intended score use, reliability, fairness, and consequences in the target population.
