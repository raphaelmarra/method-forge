# Sustainability, circular economy, energy, and process engineering

Use this catalog when environmental impact, circular value retention, resource flows, industrial conversion, energy performance, or technology economics materially affect the decision. Do not collapse environmental management, impact assessment, accounting, engineering design, and economic appraisal into one generic “sustainability methodology.”

## Environmental governance and project assessment

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISO 14001:2026 Environmental Management System | management-system standard; policy, aspects, controls, objectives, audits, and continual improvement | an organization needs auditable governance of environmental responsibilities and legal obligations | estimate one product's impact, select a process route, or claim that certification proves low impact |
| Environmental aspects-and-impacts register | governance artifact; activities, environmental interactions, significance, controls, and obligations | translate operations into controlled environmental risks and improvement priorities | use ordinal significance scores as precise damage estimates |
| Environmental Impact Assessment / ESIA | project assessment process; baseline, alternatives, predicted impacts, mitigation, monitoring, and consultation | a proposed facility, infrastructure project, or material change may create site-specific environmental or social effects | evaluate only product life-cycle burdens or justify a preselected project after design lock-in |
| Strategic Environmental Assessment | policy/plan/program assessment process | alternatives and cumulative effects should influence an upstream policy, plan, or regional program | substitute for project-level permits, site studies, or engineering controls |
| Environmental risk assessment | risk-analysis family; source–pathway–receptor scenarios and consequence/likelihood evidence | uncertain releases, exposure pathways, ecological receptors, or accident consequences require explicit risk treatment | impact categories are sufficient, or hazard is confused with realized risk |
| Mitigation hierarchy | decision heuristic; avoid, minimize, restore, then offset residual harm | biodiversity or social impacts require a sequenced response and documented residuals | offsets are used to bypass feasible avoidance or unsupported equivalence claims |

## Life-cycle and footprint methods

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISO 14040/14044 Life Cycle Assessment — LCA/ACV | standardized assessment methodology; goal/scope, functional unit, inventory, impact assessment, interpretation, and review | compare life-cycle hotspots or design alternatives with explicit boundaries and a common function | compare unlike functional units, hide allocation choices, or turn uncertain estimates into universal rankings |
| Attributional LCA | LCA modeling variant; average burdens assigned to the studied product system | describe an existing product footprint or reporting inventory under defined allocation rules | answer how markets and suppliers will change after a decision |
| Consequential LCA | LCA modeling variant; marginal system changes caused by a decision | policy or capacity decisions require induced substitutions, constraints, and market consequences | marginal suppliers and causal responses cannot be defended; do not mix silently with attributional data |
| Life Cycle Costing — LCC | economic assessment across acquisition, operation, maintenance, and end of life | alternatives shift costs across lifecycle stages or actors | environmental externalities are treated as included without explicit valuation |
| Social Life Cycle Assessment — S-LCA | impact-assessment framework; social and socioeconomic risks across value chains | labor, community, human-rights, or distributional effects are material | sparse generic databases are presented as proof about a specific supplier or site |
| Product carbon footprint | accounting method; cradle-defined greenhouse-gas inventory per functional unit | product climate claims or design choices require ISO 14067/GHG Product Standard consistency | treat carbon as the whole environmental profile or compare incompatible boundaries/data years |
| Organizational GHG inventory | accounting standard; scopes 1, 2, and relevant value-chain categories | establish a corporate emissions baseline, targets, and accountable reduction plan | allocate corporate totals to a product without a product method, or call avoided emissions an inventory reduction |
| Scope 3 inventory | value-chain GHG accounting method | purchased goods, transport, use, end of life, or investments dominate organizational emissions | supplier-specific precision is claimed from spend factors or double counting is ignored |
| Water footprint — ISO 14046 | life-cycle impact assessment focused on water use and water-related impacts | location, scarcity, quality, and life-cycle water consequences matter | report volume alone as impact or aggregate basins without spatial context |
| Environmental Product Declaration — EPD with Product Category Rules | standardized disclosure artifact | comparable category rules, verification, and transparent business-to-business disclosure are required | use an EPD as an ecolabel, performance guarantee, or comparison across different PCRs |
| Critical review / independent verification | assurance mechanism | comparative public claims, high-impact decisions, or complex allocation/model choices require independent challenge | reviewer independence, scope, competence, and unresolved limitations are omitted |

## Circularity and material-flow methods

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISO 59004:2024 | circular-economy guidance framework; vocabulary, principles, implementation direction | align stakeholders on circular-economy concepts and organizational transition | treat guidance as certification or as proof of environmental benefit |
| ISO 59010:2024 | value-model and value-network transition guidance | redesign business models, partnerships, reverse flows, and retained-value mechanisms | focus is only measuring product impact or optimizing one internal process |
| ISO 59020:2024 | circularity measurement and assessment standard | define system boundaries, collect data, and assess circularity performance consistently | infer lower total environmental impact from a circularity score alone |
| Material Flow Analysis — MFA | accounting/modeling method; stocks and flows in mass units across a defined system | locate leakage, accumulation, recovery potential, and data gaps at product, facility, city, or economy scale | monetary flows or environmental damage are the only question; mass conservation is not enforced |
| Economy-Wide Material Flow Accounts — EW-MFA | macro accounting framework; extraction, trade, consumption, and material footprint | compare national/regional resource metabolism or support SDG/resource policy | design a plant or infer product-level causality from economy aggregates |
| Material Flow Cost Accounting — MFCA, ISO 14051/14052 | management-accounting method; physical material losses plus associated costs | waste and yield losses hide energy, processing, and disposal costs inside conventional accounting | externalities or lifecycle impacts are assumed to be captured automatically |
| Substance Flow Analysis — SFA | MFA variant tracking a selected chemical, nutrient, contaminant, or element | toxicity, nutrient cycles, contamination, or recovery depends on one substance | total material efficiency is the question and the selected substance is not representative |
| Industrial symbiosis mapping | network-design method; waste/by-product/energy exchange opportunities | colocated organizations can exchange technically compatible residual streams | geographic proximity substitutes for quality, continuity, liability, or commercial validation |
| Circular design / value-retention hierarchy | design strategy family; durability, repair, reuse, refurbishment, remanufacture, and recycling choices | intervention timing and preservation of embodied value affect the business model | assume recycling is always preferable to life extension or ignore rebound and collection rates |
| Chain of custody and mass-balance models — ISO 22095 | traceability/accounting models for identity preserved, segregated, controlled blending, mass balance, or book-and-claim claims | physical or certificate claims cross multiple organizations and transformation steps | claim exact physical content when the chosen model only supports proportional attribution |
| Reverse-logistics network design | supply-chain method; collection, sorting, consolidation, recovery, and disposition network | returned quantity, quality, timing, and recovery routes drive viability | model returns as ordinary forward demand or omit incentives and contamination; see `16-supply-chain-logistics.md` |

## Process and energy engineering

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Process synthesis and flowsheet development | engineering methodology; unit operations, recycles, separations, utilities, and control concept | convert a chemistry or transformation concept into a coherent process route | equipment is selected before material properties, reactions, hazards, and balances are known |
| Mass and energy balance | conservation model and foundational assurance artifact | quantify feed, product, loss, recycle, utility, conversion, and yield under defined states | allow unexplained residuals, mix wet/dry bases, or extrapolate steady state to transients |
| Block Flow Diagram / Process Flow Diagram / P&ID | representation ladder; concept blocks, process streams/equipment, then piping/instrument/control detail | communicate process structure at the fidelity required by the current phase | use a high-level diagram for fabrication, operability review, or safety isolation decisions |
| Process simulation | numerical modeling technique; thermodynamic, kinetic, separation, recycle, and utility predictions | coupled process behavior and design trade-offs exceed hand calculation | property models, convergence, scale assumptions, or calibration lack evidence |
| Techno-Economic Analysis — TEA | integrated engineering-economic method; flowsheet, sizing, CAPEX/OPEX, unit cost, benchmark, and sensitivities | screen commercial potential and identify technical/cost drivers of an emerging process | present early “nth-plant” economics as a bankable business plan or omit scale-up/financing/market constraints |
| Cost-estimate classification | estimating framework; class/fidelity, basis, contingency, and expected range | communicate what an estimate can support as design definition increases | provide one precise CAPEX number without estimate class, base date, scope, exclusions, and uncertainty |
| Scale-up ladder: bench → pilot → demonstration → commercial | experimental/development lifecycle | transport phenomena, fouling, control, feed variability, durability, and continuous operation must be de-risked progressively | scale by geometric ratio alone or treat one successful batch as production readiness |
| Design of Experiments — DOE | experimental design technique; factor effects, interactions, response surfaces, and uncertainty | multiple controllable process variables need efficient causal learning | observational production data with uncontrolled confounding are treated as randomized experiments |
| Pinch analysis / heat integration | process-integration method; utility targets and heat-exchanger opportunities | multiple hot/cold streams create material energy-recovery potential | temperature feasibility, operability, fouling, contamination, and capital trade-offs are ignored |
| Exergy analysis | thermodynamic diagnostic; location and magnitude of useful-energy destruction | energy quality and irreversibility matter beyond first-law efficiency | economic or environmental superiority is inferred from exergy alone |
| ISO 50001 Energy Management System | management-system standard; energy review, baselines, EnPIs, action, and continual improvement | an organization needs systematic and auditable energy-performance governance | perform a one-off equipment study or certify the efficiency of a product |
| ISO 50002 energy audit family | audit methodology; energy use, opportunities, analysis, and report | buildings or processes need a bounded diagnostic and improvement opportunity register | replace continuous management or claim savings before implementation and measurement |
| Measurement and Verification — M&V | assurance method; baseline, boundary, adjustments, metering, and verified savings | savings, incentives, or performance contracts require counterfactual comparison | compare raw before/after consumption without normalizing weather, production, or operating changes |
| Levelized cost — LCOE/LCOH/LCOS and variants | lifecycle unit-cost metric | compare technologies delivering a clearly equivalent unit under declared financing and utilization | treat a single levelized number as project NPV, system value, dispatchability, risk, or market price |
| Capacity-expansion / dispatch modeling | optimization and simulation family; portfolios, operations, constraints, and system costs | energy assets interact over time through demand, networks, storage, and policy constraints | temporal/spatial resolution or reliability constraints are too coarse for the claimed decision |
| HAZOP → LOPA → SIL verification | process-safety composition | deviations, safeguards, protection layers, and instrumented risk reduction must be evaluated | append safety analysis after design freeze or use LOPA as a substitute for hazard discovery; see `06-testing-reliability-safety-security.md` |

## Climate risk, adaptation, and resilience

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Climate risk and vulnerability assessment | risk-assessment family; hazard, exposure, vulnerability, capacity, impacts, and residual risk | climate hazards can change assets, ecosystems, livelihoods, services, or investment choices | treat a climate scenario as a local forecast or omit exposure, adaptive capacity, and uncertainty |
| Climate-resilient development | integrated planning framework | mitigation, adaptation, development, equity, and feasibility must be considered together | label any low-carbon project climate-resilient without testing trade-offs and lock-in |
| Adaptation pathways | adaptive planning method | actions have thresholds, lead times, path dependencies, and transfer points under changing conditions | propose a static adaptation list without triggers, monitoring, or option value |
| Climate stress testing | scenario-based assurance method | portfolios, infrastructure, supply chains, or services need vulnerability across plausible hazards and socioeconomic futures | report one deterministic temperature or hazard case as a full risk assessment |
| Nature-based solutions assessment | intervention appraisal family | ecosystem-based measures may reduce risk while producing biodiversity and social outcomes | count co-benefits without permanence, land-rights, maintenance, or counterfactual evidence |

## Selection boundaries and composition patterns

| Question | Primary method | Common complements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How should the organization govern environmental performance? | ISO 14001:2026 | obligations register, objectives, audits, ISO 50001 |
| What are the environmental hotspots of a product route? | LCA | uncertainty/sensitivity, critical review, TEA |
| What changes because we choose this policy or capacity? | consequential LCA | causal model, scenarios, market and energy-system model |
| Where are physical losses and stocks? | MFA/SFA | sampling, reconciliation, geospatial flow map |
| What do internal material losses cost? | MFCA | process mapping, yield/OEE, improvement experiment |
| Is the emerging process technically and economically promising? | mass/energy balance + TEA | benchmark, scale-up plan, Monte Carlo/sensitivity, LCA |
| Is the business model genuinely circular? | ISO 59010 + reverse-network model | ISO 59020, LCA, chain of custody, unit economics |
| Did an energy intervention cause savings? | M&V | metering plan, baseline model, uncertainty and independent review |

Recommended project stack for a circular conversion technology:

`feedstock envelope and contaminant/product-status characterization + mass/energy balance → flowsheet/simulation → TEA + LCA → supply/reverse-network and offtake model → early PHA/HAZID → pilot/scale-up evidence → mature-design HAZOP/LOPA/SIL as warranted → licensing gates → investment model → M&V and operating controls`

Do not let TEA validate its own technical assumptions, LCA validate its own market consequences, or a circularity metric validate environmental benefit. Use independent evidence and paired methods.

Laboratory evidence alone does not support a final commercial investment decision. At concept/laboratory maturity, authorize only a bounded learning tranche with stop/scale criteria. Commercial commitment requires decision-specific evidence for representative feedstock and duration, integrated performance and emissions/residues, product specification and legal status, supply/offtake, scale-up, safety/licensing, estimate maturity, and independent technical/commercial challenge. The exact readiness threshold is sector- and risk-specific; do not convert a TRL number directly into financial approval.

## Research anchors and status

Status checked 2026-08-12.

- [ISO 14001:2026](https://www.iso.org/standard/14001) is the current published environmental-management-system edition; ISO 14001:2015 is withdrawn.
- [ISO 14040:2006](https://www.iso.org/standard/37456.html) and [ISO 14044:2006](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html), with applicable amendments, define the LCA framework and requirements.
- [ISO 59004:2024](https://www.iso.org/standard/80648.html), [ISO 59010:2024](https://www.iso.org/standard/80649.html), and [ISO 59020:2024](https://www.iso.org/standard/80650.html) cover circular-economy principles, value-network transition, and performance assessment respectively.
- [ISO 14051:2011](https://www.iso.org/standard/50986.html) remains current for MFCA; [ISO 14052:2017](https://www.iso.org/standard/54811.html) extends implementation into supply chains.
- [GHG Protocol standards and guidance](https://ghgprotocol.org/standards-guidance) distinguish organizational, Scope 2, Scope 3, product, and project accounting purposes.
- [ISO 14067:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/71206.html) addresses product carbon footprints; [ISO 14064-1:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/66453.html) addresses organizational GHG inventories.
- [ISO 50001:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/69426.html) remains current and confirmed in 2024; the ISO 50002 parts were updated in 2025 for audit principles, buildings, and processes.
- [U.S. DOE LCA and TEA training](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/ito/life-cycle-assessment-and-techno-economic-analysis-training) provides a public implementation anchor for functional units, benchmarks, manufacturing costs, and early-stage analysis.
- [CCPS](https://ccps.aiche.org/) is the principal process-safety implementation anchor for HAZOP, LOPA, quantitative risk, and protection-layer practice.
- Use the UNEP economy-wide material-flow manuals and database for macro MFA. Label organizational circularity, product impact, and economy-wide resource accounting as different units of analysis.
- [IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/) is the primary climate-risk and response anchor; use the relevant Working Group assessment for hazards, impacts/adaptation, or mitigation rather than treating the synthesis as a project method.
