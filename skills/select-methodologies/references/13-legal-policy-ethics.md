# Legal, regulatory, public-policy, compliance, rights, and ethics methods

Use this catalog when authority, jurisdiction, public intervention, rights, compliance obligations, or ethical governance determine the valid method. Legal research supports issue analysis; it is not legal representation or a substitute for qualified counsel in consequential matters. For crisis leadership, incident coordination, emergency management, continuity, and recovery, use `32-crisis-continuity-emergency-management.md`.

## Legal and regulatory research

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Doctrinal legal research | authority-based research method; issue, governing sources, interpretation, application, contrary authority, and conclusion | determine what law applies in a jurisdiction as of a date | infer practical compliance from statute text alone or present agent output as counsel's advice |
| IRAC / CREAC | legal-analysis writing structure | organize issue, rule, explanation, application, and conclusion after research | use as the research method, omit contrary authority, or force complex issues into one syllogism |
| Authority and citator/version checking | assurance method | validity depends on hierarchy, amendments, repeal, judicial treatment, commencement, and consolidation | cite a convenient unofficial copy or assume “latest search result” is in force |
| Legislative history / travaux préparatoires | interpretive research method | applicable doctrine permits preparatory material to clarify purpose, ambiguity, or treaty meaning | override clear controlling text where the jurisdiction does not allow it |
| Comparative legal research | comparative method; functional and contextual comparison across legal systems | alternative regulatory designs or cross-border obligations must be understood | transplant a rule without institutions, enforcement, legal culture, definitions, and scope |
| Empirical / socio-legal research | research family examining law in practice | implementation, enforcement, access, behavior, distribution, and outcomes are the question | empirical prevalence determines normative validity or anecdote represents a system |
| Legal applicability and obligation register | governance artifact; authority, obligation, applicability, owner, control, evidence, effective date, and update trigger | multiple rules must become accountable operational controls | a checklist hides interpretation, conflicts, exemptions, local permits, or changes |
| Regulatory mapping | systems artifact; agencies, instruments, approvals, dependencies, fees, lead times, appeals, and evidence | a product/facility/business crosses regulatory regimes and authorities | list generic laws without mapping the exact activity, actor, location, product, and lifecycle phase |
| Licensing/permitting pathway analysis | implementation method | approvals, submissions, tests, inspections, sequencing, and critical path shape feasibility | assume certification, registration, homologation, authorization, and license are synonyms |
| Legal-change monitoring | continuous-control method | obligations or official interpretations can change after the initial analysis | rely on alerts without a relevance/applicability review and owned remediation |

For every legal conclusion state: `jurisdiction | issue | law-as-of date | controlling source | applicability facts | contrary/uncertain authority | conclusion state | counsel/authority gate`.

## Compliance and legal-risk management

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISO 37301 compliance management system | certifiable management-system standard | an organization needs enduring governance for compliance obligations, culture, controls, reporting, investigation, audit, and improvement | answer one legal question or claim certification proves every act lawful |
| ISO 37302 compliance-effectiveness guidance | assurance guidance | evaluate whether implemented compliance arrangements operate and achieve intended outcomes | substitute for ISO 37301 requirements, a legal opinion, or independent enforcement evidence |
| ISO 31022 legal-risk guidance | specialist risk guidance | integrate legal uncertainty, events, consequences, treatments, and monitoring into risk management | average mandatory legality or non-derogable duties into a risk appetite score |
| Compliance risk assessment | risk-analysis method | prioritize monitoring, controls, training, due diligence, and testing across obligations and scenarios | low likelihood is used to waive a mandatory requirement or enforcement visibility equals actual risk |
| Control–obligation–evidence matrix | representation and assurance artifact | each material obligation must map to preventive/detective controls, owners, tests, and retained evidence | policy existence is treated as operating effectiveness |
| ISO 19011 management-system audit guidance | audit-process guidance | design risk-based audit programs, competence, evidence, findings, and follow-up | claim certification authority or legal privilege, or let process owners audit themselves on high-risk controls |
| Internal investigation protocol | controlled investigative workflow | allegations, misconduct, retaliation, evidence, privacy, independence, and remediation require consistent handling | launch without authority/scope, promise impossible confidentiality, or prejudge culpability |
| Anti-bribery management — ISO 37001 | specialist management-system standard | bribery risk justifies targeted policy, due diligence, financial/nonfinancial controls, reporting, and review | treat anti-bribery as the whole compliance system or certification as absence of bribery |
| Third-party compliance due diligence | risk-based lifecycle | intermediaries, suppliers, partners, acquisition targets, or beneficiaries create legal/integrity exposure | questionnaire and sanctions screen substitute for ownership, reputation, capability, transaction, and ongoing monitoring |

## Public policy and program evaluation

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regulatory Impact Assessment — RIA/AIR | ex ante policy-analysis process; problem, baseline, options including no action, impacts, consultation, implementation, and monitoring | regulation is genuinely undecided and proportional analysis can affect the choice | preferred rule is fixed and the document merely rationalizes it after the fact |
| Post-Implementation Review / ex post regulatory evaluation | retrospective evaluation | determine whether a rule remains necessary, effective, efficient, coherent, and proportionate | label it ex ante RIA or ignore adaptation, enforcement, and unintended effects |
| Theory of Change / logic model | causal-program representation | expose mechanism, assumptions, outputs, outcomes, indicators, and rival explanations | treat arrows as identified causal effects |
| Monitoring and results framework | measurement/governance artifact | recurrent indicators, baselines, targets, responsibilities, data quality, and decision triggers are needed | monitoring alone answers attribution, mechanisms, or value-for-money questions |
| OECD DAC evaluation criteria | evaluative criteria; relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, impact, sustainability | frame judgments for development/public interventions with context-sensitive questions | use as an end-to-end evaluation method or scorecard detached from stakeholder values |
| Process / implementation evaluation | evaluation method | fidelity, reach, dose, adaptations, context, mechanisms, and bottlenecks explain delivery | infer impact solely because implementation was faithful |
| Experimental / quasi-experimental impact evaluation | causal-design family | estimate an intervention's counterfactual effect under defensible assumptions | the real question is implementation or mechanisms, or identification assumptions fail; see `08-decision-optimization-causality.md` |
| Realist evaluation | theory-driven evaluation | determine what works, for whom, in which contexts, and through what mechanisms | one homogeneous average effect is the only decision or context–mechanism claims lack evidence |
| Contribution analysis + process tracing | theory-based causal contribution method | multiple causes prevent simple attribution but diagnostic evidence can strengthen or weaken a contribution claim | convert plausibility into an effect size or ignore rival explanations |
| Public appraisal / Value for Money | economic/public-value assessment | compare social costs, benefits, risks, distribution, additionality, and alternatives | use private ROI as the only public criterion or monetize rights and hard constraints away |
| Test and Learn / adaptive policy | adaptive implementation/evaluation approach | uncertainty is material, rollout can be staged safely, and feedback can change policy | experimentation exposes groups to unacceptable harm or operational changes destroy inference without documentation |
| Analytical Quality Assurance — AQA | assurance framework | models and analyses materially influence public decisions and need proportional independent challenge | a review checklist substitutes for competent replication, data checks, and decision accountability |

| Logical Framework Approach / Logframe | program-design and monitoring method | inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes, assumptions, indicators, and responsibilities need one testable project logic | complete a matrix as paperwork, hide uncertainty in assumptions, or treat logical links as causal proof |
| Results-Based Management | management and learning framework | resources, outputs, outcomes, indicators, risks, and adaptive decisions must connect across a program cycle | reduce complex change to target compliance or reward reporting volume over learning |
| Outcome Mapping | participatory planning, monitoring, and evaluation method | change depends on behavior, relationships, and boundary partners beyond one agency's control | claim population impact from observed behavioral markers or ignore contribution and context |
| Most Significant Change | qualitative outcome-learning technique | diverse stakeholders need to surface unexpected, valued, or contested changes and explain why they matter | use selected stories as prevalence estimates, suppress negative cases, or replace systematic monitoring |
| Evidence-to-Decision framework | deliberative decision artifact/process | evidence, benefits, harms, values, equity, acceptability, feasibility, and implementation must be made explicit | treat a recommendation as a mechanical consequence of evidence certainty |

## Ethics, human rights, and responsible innovation

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Human Rights-Based Approach — HRBA | program/governance approach | rights-holders, duty-bearers, participation, equality, accountability, and remedy should shape intervention design | generic stakeholder engagement is relabeled HRBA without applicable rights and duties |
| Human Rights Impact Assessment — HRIA | impact-assessment method | a project, policy, product, or business activity may affect rights across groups and value chains | perform only reputational ESG scoring or allow aggregate benefit to waive rights |
| Human-rights due diligence — UNGP/OECD RBC | ongoing risk-based lifecycle | identify, prevent, mitigate, track, communicate, and remediate adverse impacts connected to operations and relationships | one pre-contract screen or code-of-conduct signature is called due diligence |
| Responsible Innovation — AREA | governance framework; anticipate, reflect, engage, act | research/technology direction should adapt to uncertainty, values, consequences, and affected voices | workshops create no design, governance, or stop/modify decisions |
| Value Sensitive Design — VSD | design methodology | values and stakeholder tensions must become technical/organizational requirements and empirical inquiry | designer intuition defines universal values or ethical design substitutes for rights/legal review |
| Ethical Impact Assessment | structured ethics assessment | benefits, harms, autonomy, fairness, governance, oversight, and mitigation need evidence before deployment | principles checklist or self-assessment approves its own high-risk system |
| Research ethics review | independent governance/assurance | human participants, identifiable data, intervention, deception, vulnerability, or material risk require ethics review/consent safeguards | use approval as proof the study is scientifically valid or bypass jurisdictional requirements |
| Precautionary approach | decision principle | plausible serious/irreversible harm exists despite scientific uncertainty and proportionate preventive action is feasible | demand zero uncertainty, prohibit all innovation, or invoke precaution without alternatives/evidence review |
| Structural–process–outcome rights indicators | measurement framework | commitment, effort, results, inequality, and accountability must be observed | indicators replace legal judgment/testimony or aggregates hide excluded groups |

Hard rights, legal duties, safety limits, and remedy are constraints, not criteria to average away in MCDA or benefit–cost totals.

## Composition patterns

### Legal/compliance operating stack

`jurisdiction + issue → controlling authority/version research → applicability and obligation register → legal-risk scenarios → controls/owners/evidence → monitoring/investigation → effectiveness evaluation → independent audit → corrective action + change monitoring`

Escalate consequential interpretation, filings, strategy, rights, liability, or representation to qualified counsel or the competent authority.

### Regulatory-policy lifecycle

`problem/baseline → options including no action → proportional ex ante RIA + consultation + rights/distribution analysis → independent scrutiny → decision record → implementation/monitoring → process/impact/VfM evaluation → ex post review → retain/amend/repeal`

### Public-program evaluation

`decision/use → Theory of Change + rivals → monitoring baseline → process evaluation + causal impact or contribution/realist design → distribution/rights + VfM → AQA/independent review → dissemination, decision, and learning`

### Responsible-innovation stack

`applicable rights/duties → affected-party map → AREA anticipation/reflection/engagement → VSD/domain design → HRIA/EIA → requirements, mitigations, and remedy → independent safety/security/privacy/ethics gate → monitored deployment and reassessment`

## Research anchors and status

Status checked 2026-08-12. Always live-check jurisdictional sources and effective dates.

- [OECD Regulatory Impact Assessment principles](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/regulatory-impact-assessment_7a9638cb-en.html) are the canonical cross-jurisdiction RIA anchor; local law and procedure control.
- [USAID Logical Framework guide](https://www.usaid.gov/sites/default/files/2022-05/The-Logical-Framework-A-Managers-Guide.pdf) is a source anchor for the Logframe as a design-and-evaluation aid, not a guarantee of causality.
- [IDRC Outcome Mapping](https://idrc-crdi.ca/en/books/outcome-mapping-building-learning-and-reflection-development-programs) anchors behavior/relationship-centered planning, monitoring, and contribution learning in complex development programs.
- [ISO 37301:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/75080.html) plus Amendment 1:2024 remains the compliance-management requirements standard; ISO 37302:2025 provides effectiveness guidance.
- ISO 19011:2026 is the current management-system audit guidance; do not cite the superseded 2018 edition as current.
- [UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Publications/GuidingPrinciplesBusinessHR_EN.pdf) and the 2023 OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises are authoritative general responsible-business anchors; apply current jurisdictional duties.
- [WMA Declaration of Helsinki 2024](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/) is the current version for medical research ethics involving human participants.
- Route crisis, continuity, incident, emergency, disaster-recovery, and resilience methods to `32-crisis-continuity-emergency-management.md`.
