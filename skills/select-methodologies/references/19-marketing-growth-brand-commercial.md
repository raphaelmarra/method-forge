# Marketing, growth, brand, and commercial activation

Use this catalog when the decision concerns whom to serve, how to position an offer, build and govern a brand, enter a market, select channels, acquire or retain customers, allocate media, or measure incremental commercial impact. Market research describes demand and choices; marketing turns that evidence into coordinated market action.

## Marketing strategy and positioning

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Market orientation | organizational principle/capability; systematic customer, competitor, and cross-functional learning | strategy must respond continuously to changing customers and competitors | “customer-led” excuses copying requests, ignoring economics, or abandoning technical/regulatory judgment |
| Segmentation–Targeting–Positioning — STP | strategy backbone; actionable segments, target choice, and differentiated position | heterogeneous needs, economics, reachability, or behavior justify distinct choices | demographic labels lack different action, economics, or evidence; research mechanics belong to `12-finance-market-audit-procurement.md` |
| Ideal Customer Profile — ICP | B2B targeting artifact; firmographic, operational, need, value, readiness, and exclusion criteria | sales and marketing need a shared account-selection rule | a fictional “perfect customer” is inferred from a few wins or confuses user, buyer, approver, and beneficiary |
| Buying committee / decision-unit mapping | B2B stakeholder method; roles, criteria, influence, process, evidence, and objections | several people shape problem recognition, approval, purchase, adoption, and renewal | one persona represents an organization or titles substitute for observed authority and incentives |
| Jobs-to-be-Done / circumstance segmentation | discovery and segmentation method | desired progress, context, barriers, and trade-offs explain choice better than demographics | interview stories are treated as market size or every difference becomes a segment; canonical discovery card is in `12-finance-market-audit-procurement.md` |
| Category and frame analysis | strategic framing technique; category alternatives, reference frame, entry points, and conventions | buyers do not understand what the offer is or compare it against the wrong alternatives | inventing a category substitutes for demand, distribution, proof, and repeated buyer recognition |
| Positioning statement + reason-to-believe | strategy artifact; audience, frame, differentiated value, proof, and constraints | product, sales, brand, and channels need one testable market promise | generic superlatives, internal features, or unsupported claims replace customer-relative value |
| Value Proposition Canvas | structured hypothesis artifact | customer jobs/pains/gains and offer features need an explicit fit hypothesis | canvas completion is called validation or prioritization ignores severity, frequency, alternatives, and willingness to change |
| Perceptual / preference mapping | research/visualization technique | relative brand/offer perceptions or attributes need empirical comparison | axes are selected for a desired story, sparse stated opinions imply behavior, or distance is overinterpreted |
| Competitive alternative analysis | strategic analysis; direct, indirect, status-quo, workaround, and nonconsumption alternatives | positioning and sales must reflect what customers actually choose instead | competitor feature grids ignore switching, distribution, trust, total cost, and doing nothing |
| Marketing objectives and strategy map | governance artifact; outcome, audience, behavior, mechanism, intervention, and measure | activities need traceability to business and customer outcomes | awareness, traffic, leads, or content volume are accepted without a causal path to value |
| Marketing mix — 4Ps/7Ps | coordination framework | product/offer, price, place, promotion, people, process, and physical evidence must align | use as a research method, fixed checklist, or proof that the chosen mix is effective |

## Brand strategy and governance

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brand purpose, promise, and values hierarchy | governance artifact | identity, behavior, experience, and claims need coherent principles | aspirational words contradict operating choices or become an untestable moral claim |
| Brand identity system | strategy/representation system; verbal, visual, behavioral, and experiential identifiers | repeated recognition and coherent execution are needed across touchpoints | logo redesign substitutes for positioning, product quality, or distribution |
| Brand architecture | portfolio method; branded house, house of brands, endorsed, or hybrid relationships | multiple offers, acquisitions, audiences, or reputations create naming and investment choices | organizational chart becomes customer-facing architecture or complexity exceeds buyer relevance |
| Portfolio roles and migration | portfolio governance; role, investment, overlap, migration, and retirement plan | several brands/offers compete for resources or confuse customers | every legacy name is preserved or consolidation ignores equity, contracts, search, channel, and transition risk |
| Distinctive brand-asset audit | empirical brand method; recognition, uniqueness, fame, and usage rules for assets | buyers need reliable memory cues across fragmented exposure | aesthetic preference or internal recognition proves market distinctiveness |
| Brand codes / memory-structure plan | implementation pattern | consistent sensory and verbal cues should compound recognition over time | rigid sameness blocks usability, accessibility, local adaptation, or clear product differentiation |
| Brand tracking | longitudinal measurement system | awareness, salience, consideration, associations, usage, trust, and preference must be monitored consistently | one survey wave proves causality or changing samples/questions fabricate a trend |
| Brand evaluation — ISO 20671 family | evaluation/governance framework | brand inputs, strength, performance, implementation, and reporting need a structured evaluation | certification theater, one composite score, or brand evaluation is confused with monetary valuation |
| Monetary brand valuation — ISO 10668 | financial valuation framework | transaction, licensing, reporting, dispute, or investment decisions require monetary brand value | use marketing metrics alone, hide assumptions, or present a draft revision as current; financial modeling belongs to `12` |
| Semiotic / cultural analysis | qualitative interpretation method | signs, narratives, codes, and category meanings materially shape perception | analyst interpretation substitutes for audience research, prevalence, or behavioral validation |
| Naming and verbal-identity evaluation | design/research workflow | names, terminology, tone, and message hierarchy need linguistic, strategic, legal, and user checks | preference voting alone or creative appeal bypasses trademark, pronunciation, cultural, accessibility, and search constraints |
| Message architecture and claim–proof matrix | representation/assurance artifact | different audiences and funnel stages need prioritized claims with evidence and permitted wording | every benefit is communicated at once or unsupported performance/social/environmental claims are published |

Brand strategy determines desired meaning and memory; visual communication realizes it. Route identity execution, information hierarchy, and accessibility to `20-design-experience-communication.md`; route trademark, advertising, privacy, and regulated claims to `13-legal-policy-ethics.md`.

## Go-to-market, routes, and commercial activation

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Go-to-market — GTM design | cross-functional launch/growth backbone; target, use case, offer, proof, price, channel, sales motion, service, and metrics | a new offer, segment, geography, or business model must reach repeatable adoption | launch calendar or campaign alone is called GTM; product readiness, service, supply, economics, and regulation are open |
| Beachhead / wedge strategy | sequencing strategy | resources are constrained and one reachable segment/use case can produce learning, proof, and expansion | tiny niche has no adjacency, reference value, acceptable economics, or defensibility |
| Route-to-market / channel design | commercial systems method; direct, distributor, marketplace, partner, retail, inside/outside sales roles and economics | availability, coverage, control, service, data, working capital, and conflict differ by route | choose channels by popularity or gross revenue while ignoring margin, incentives, ownership, and service burden |
| Channel unit economics | economic model | acquisition, discounts, commissions, logistics, returns, enablement, credit, and support vary by channel | compare revenue without contribution, working capital, cannibalization, or partner dependence; canonical economics belong to `12` |
| Partner ecosystem design | operating-model method | complementary capabilities, referrals, implementation, distribution, or platforms can accelerate adoption | “partnership” lacks exchanged value, qualification, enablement, data rights, accountability, and exit |
| Sales-motion selection | commercial design; self-serve, product-led, inside, field, channel, or hybrid motion | complexity, contract value, risk, buying process, onboarding, and support justify different motions | copy SaaS labels or use high-touch sales to compensate for unclear value and unusable onboarding |
| Account-Based Marketing — ABM | B2B orchestration approach | high-value accounts have identifiable buying groups and justify coordinated personalized effort | low-value/high-volume markets, weak account data, or personalization is superficial spam |
| Launch readiness / commercialization gate | assurance gate | product, operations, legal claims, channel, service, instrumentation, inventory, and rollback must be ready together | date pressure, campaign spend, or executive enthusiasm approves unresolved critical dependencies |
| Adoption funnel / customer journey | representation and measurement model | stages, transitions, frictions, actors, and interventions need shared visibility | a universal linear funnel hides loops, offline steps, multiple actors, delayed conversion, and noncustomers |
| Sales enablement evidence system | commercial capability; playbooks, proof, objection handling, qualification, and feedback | complex offers require consistent evidence and field learning | scripts replace judgment or marketing collateral makes claims unsupported by product and operations |
| Win/loss program | commercial learning method | repeated decisions can reveal alternatives, criteria, process, proof gaps, and segment patterns | salesperson recollection is ground truth or interviews exclude no-decision and churn; canonical card is in `12` |
| Pilot-to-scale commercial design | staged adoption method | customers need bounded proof before rollout and learning can reduce implementation/value risk | free pilots lack success criteria, buyer commitment, production path, economics, or stop/convert decision |

## Acquisition, lifecycle, content, and growth

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reach–frequency planning | media planning method | brand or demand outcomes depend on how many relevant people are exposed and how often | impressions substitute for deduplicated reach, attention, context, creative quality, or outcome |
| Media planning and portfolio allocation | planning/optimization family | channels differ in reach, cost, response, saturation, timing, and strategic role | last-click ROAS directly sets budget or historical allocation constrains discovery without challenge |
| Creative strategy / message–audience matrix | design hypothesis system | multiple audiences, needs, contexts, formats, and objections require explicit creative hypotheses | produce variants without a learning question or platform metrics become the only success criterion |
| Creative testing | experiment/research family | message, concept, format, or execution choices can be compared before or during deployment | preference tests predict sales, multiple changes prevent diagnosis, or winner selection ignores uncertainty and wear-out |
| Content strategy | governance method; audience needs, topics, formats, distribution, lifecycle, ownership, and measures | content must support discovery, evaluation, use, trust, and retention over time | publishing volume, trend chasing, or SEO traffic exists without an audience decision or business role |
| Content design / structured content | information-design method | people must find, understand, and act on accurate content across channels and reuse contexts | copywriting polish compensates for wrong service, missing information, or inaccessible structure; design ownership in `20` |
| SEO demand and discoverability workflow | acquisition method; query intent, technical access, information architecture, content, links, and outcome measurement | organic search is a material route through which audiences express needs | ranking/traffic is the objective, doorway content is created, or search demand is mistaken for total market demand |
| Paid-search / intent-capture design | activation method | expressed demand can be matched to relevant offer and landing experience with measurable cost | bidding manufactures demand, brand/nonbrand effects are mixed, or platform attribution proves incrementality |
| Social/community strategy | relationship/distribution method | participation, peer exchange, creators, advocacy, or community support the category and offer | follower count, posting cadence, or unpaid moderation is called community value |
| Referral / advocacy program design | growth mechanism | satisfied participants can credibly introduce others and incentives preserve trust/economics | incentives create fraud, adverse selection, disclosure issues, or referrals mask weak organic retention |
| Growth-loop modeling | systems representation; action creates an output that increases future acquisition, activation, or value | product, content, data, collaboration, or referrals can generate a reinforcing mechanism | circular diagram lacks measurable conversion, capacity constraints, decay, abuse controls, or causal evidence |
| Lifecycle marketing | customer-state orchestration | onboarding, activation, use, expansion, renewal, lapse, and win-back need state-specific intervention | calendar blasts ignore state, consent, frequency, relevance, and service causes of churn |
| Cohort retention analysis | longitudinal measurement method | adoption and value persistence differ by start period, segment, channel, or intervention | aggregate retention hides mix shifts or descriptive curves prove why customers remain |
| RFM / behavioral segmentation | prioritization technique | recency, frequency, value, engagement, or lifecycle states support differentiated contact/service | past spending alone defines future value or vulnerable customers receive manipulative targeting |
| Customer Lifetime Value — CLV/LTV | forward economic model | acquisition, retention, service, and channel decisions require expected contribution over a customer relationship | revenue LTV ignores margin, discounting, survival, returns, service, selection, or uncertainty; financial ownership in `12` |
| CAC and payback | acquisition economics metrics | growth needs a comparable view of incremental acquisition cost and recovery time | blended CAC hides organic/paid mix, salaries and incentives, cohort quality, working capital, or causality |
| Churn / renewal diagnostic | mixed-method learning workflow | loss, downgrade, nonuse, or renewal failures need mechanism-specific interventions | cancellation reason dropdown is causal truth or discounts treat product/service failures as price problems |
| CRM contact-policy design | operational decision system | eligibility, priority, channel, frequency, next-best action, consent, suppression, and escalation need governance | optimization maximizes clicks while creating fatigue, discrimination, privacy harm, or channel conflict |

## Pricing, offer, and promotion interfaces

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Offer architecture | commercial design; core value, scope, bundles, tiers, terms, proof, risk reversal, and exclusions | buyers struggle to compare, adopt, or understand what is included | packaging manipulates confusion or bundles conceal poor unit economics and service constraints |
| Good–better–best / tiering | offer structure technique | distinct needs, willingness, service levels, or features justify meaningful tiers | decoy abuse, artificial feature withholding, or tiers lack operational and economic separation |
| Bundling / unbundling analysis | pricing/offer technique | complementarity, transaction cost, adoption, or willingness differs across components | average willingness, forced bundles, cannibalization, tax/regulatory, and service cost are ignored |
| Promotion architecture | activation method; objective, audience, mechanism, duration, funding, guardrail, and post-period analysis | temporary incentives can trigger trial, inventory movement, switching, or channel action | permanent discounting trains delay, attracts unprofitable demand, violates price rules, or baseline pull-forward is ignored |
| Price experimentation | causal pricing method | prices or terms can be varied ethically and legally with adequate units and spillover control | simultaneous exposure creates unfairness/arbitrage, low power, strategic response, or reputational/regulatory risk dominates |
| Van Westendorp price-sensitivity meter | stated-perception heuristic | early exploratory language around cheap/expensive thresholds may guide later research | output is called optimal willingness-to-pay, demand curve, elasticity, or causal revenue response |
| Gabor–Granger | stated-purchase-intent pricing technique | candidate price points need exploratory response under a credible offer | hypothetical intent, anchoring, sample bias, and no competitive response are ignored |
| Conjoint / discrete choice | preference experiment | attribute and price trade-offs require structured stated choices | choice design is weak or stated adoption is treated as realized demand; canonical research card is in `12` |

Pricing research and economic modeling are canonically owned by `12-finance-market-audit-procurement.md`; this catalog owns how price, packaging, promotion, positioning, and channels are activated coherently.

## Marketing measurement and causal assurance

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Measurement framework / metric tree | representation and governance artifact | business outcome, customer behavior, intervention, leading indicators, guardrails, definitions, and ownership need alignment | dashboard volume replaces decisions or platform definitions are silently mixed |
| Funnel conversion analysis | descriptive diagnostic | stage counts, rates, time, leakage, and segment variation identify where to investigate | conversion correlations establish causes or stages omit eligibility, delay, duplication, and offline movement |
| Randomized user-level lift test | causal experiment | treatment assignment, holdout, identity/consent, interference, power, and outcome measurement are defensible | platform-reported attribution substitutes for randomized estimand or exposure noncompliance is ignored |
| Geo experiment / matched-market test | causal experiment | media or commercial interventions can vary by geography with comparable markets and spillover controls | few heterogeneous regions, mobility/media leakage, concurrent shocks, or low power invalidate the contrast |
| Conversion-lift / holdout study | incrementality design | determine conversions caused by a campaign/platform beyond baseline | self-selected exposed/unexposed groups, opaque eligibility, cross-device gaps, and short windows are ignored |
| Marketing Mix Modeling — MMM | aggregate econometric causal-model family | multi-channel budget effects must be estimated with sufficient time/geo variation, controls, priors, and uncertainty | predictive fit is called causal validity, collinearity/targeting/confounding dominate, or granular optimization exceeds data resolution |
| Experiment-calibrated MMM | hybrid assurance pattern | experiments can anchor priors or calibrate selected channel effects and reconcile aggregate planning | incompatible estimands, populations, periods, or execution are combined mechanically |
| Multi-touch attribution — MTA | path-credit model family | deterministic/probabilistic journey credit is operationally useful within observable digital paths | identity loss, selection, walled gardens, offline effects, and no counterfactual make credit look causal |
| Last-/first-touch attribution | bookkeeping heuristic | a simple operational convention is explicitly needed for routing or reporting | budget, ROI, or incrementality decisions depend on it |
| Causal impact / synthetic control | quasi-experimental family | intervention timing and unaffected comparison series support a defensible counterfactual | concurrent changes, anticipation, interference, or unstable relation make the synthetic comparison invalid; canonical causal methods in `08` |
| Incremental CAC / iROAS | causal economic metrics | acquisition or revenue caused by intervention must be related to incremental spend | attributed conversions/revenue, gross sales, or short-term lift ignores margin, retention, substitution, and uncertainty |
| Brand-lift study | experimental/survey measurement | exposure effects on awareness, recall, association, or intent need a declared survey estimand | stated lift proves sales, brand health, long-term effect, or platform-general impact |
| Search/share-of-search trend | demand/salience proxy | brand/category query behavior can complement other longitudinal evidence | search volume is market share, causality, or universal mental availability |
| Marketing data contract and taxonomy | data-governance artifact | campaigns, audiences, creatives, spend, exposure, conversion, revenue, consent, and versions must reconcile across systems | naming convention alone fixes identity, missingness, platform bias, or causal validity |
| Pre/post campaign review | learning/governance workflow | hypotheses, execution, delivery, outcomes, counterfactual evidence, anomalies, and next actions must be preserved | retrospective storytelling selects favorable metrics or judges strategy only by delivery |

Measurement hierarchy: delivery (`was it served?`) → attention/engagement (`was there observable interaction?`) → behavior/outcome (`what happened?`) → incrementality (`what happened because of it?`) → economics (`was the incremental effect worth its full cost?`). Never collapse these claims.

## Composition patterns

### Build a go-to-market strategy

`decision and growth objective → market/competitor evidence from 12 → STP + ICP/buying committee → positioning + claim–proof → offer/price architecture → route-to-market + sales motion → launch readiness gate → instrumented beachhead → adoption/retention evidence → staged expansion`

### Build and measure a B2B demand system

`account universe + buying groups → need/category entry points → content and proof system → channel/ABM orchestration → qualification and sales handoff → CRM state/contact policy → cohort pipeline and revenue outcomes → holdout/geo tests where feasible → CAC/payback/retention review`

### Govern brand growth

`positioning and portfolio role → identity/distinctive-asset audit → message and experience system → reach/distribution plan → longitudinal brand + behavior tracking → creative/incrementality tests → brand evaluation → investment and migration decisions`

### Allocate a marketing portfolio

`business outcome + metric tree → reconciled spend/exposure/outcome data → experiments by feasible channel → MMM with causal controls and uncertainty → calibration/triangulation → constrained scenario allocation → staged budget change → post-change validation`

### Improve lifecycle retention

`customer-state model → cohort/behavior diagnosis → service/product causes → segment-specific intervention → consent/frequency guardrails → randomized or phased test → incremental retention/contribution → contact-policy update → adverse-effect monitoring`

## Research anchors and status

Status checked 2026-08-12. Marketing practices and platform measurement change quickly; verify the exact platform, jurisdiction, privacy basis, definitions, and current documentation at use time.

- [American Marketing Association](https://www.ama.org/topics/brand-and-branding/) provides discipline vocabulary for brands, identity, equity, positioning, and related marketing concepts; definitions are not execution evidence.
- [ISO 20671-1:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/81739.html) and ISO 20671-2:2023 are current brand-evaluation principles and implementation/reporting standards. ISO 20671:2019 is withdrawn.
- [ISO 10668:2010](https://www.iso.org/standard/46032.html) remains the published monetary brand-valuation standard, confirmed in 2023; a second edition is an early work item in 2026 and must be labeled draft/development.
- [ICC/ESOMAR Code 2025](https://standards.esomar.org/assets/documents/icc-esomar-code-2025.pdf) and [ISO 20252:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/73671.html) anchor ethical and quality requirements for market/opinion/social research services; verify the ongoing ISO revision.
- [Google Meridian](https://developers.google.com/meridian) and [Meta Robyn](https://facebookexperimental.github.io/Robyn/docs/welcome/) are transparent implementation ecosystems for MMM, not automatic causal proof. Meridian explicitly distinguishes causal goals from predictive fit and recommends experimental calibration where feasible.
- [IAB Modernizing MMM guidance](https://www.iab.com/guidelines/modernizing-mmm-best-practices-for-marketers/) and [IAB incremental-measurement guidance](https://www.iab.com/guidelines/guidelines-for-incremental-measurement-in-commerce-media/) are current industry anchors; campaign-data standards released for public comment in 2026 remain draft.
- Use controlling consumer, advertising, competition, privacy, direct-marketing, pricing, promotion, environmental/health-claim, and sector rules for the target jurisdiction. A marketing method cannot authorize a claim or data use.
