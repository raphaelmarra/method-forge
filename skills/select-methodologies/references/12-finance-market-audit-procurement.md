# Finance, investment, market intelligence, audit, and procurement

Use this catalog when capital allocation, commercial evidence, financial controls, investigations, supplier selection, or technology acquisition determine the decision. Keep financial appraisal, delivery control, market research, audit, fraud investigation, and due diligence as distinct method families.

## Finance and investment economics

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Options-based business case / Five Case Model | appraisal/governance backbone; strategic, economic, commercial, financial, and management cases | a material proposal needs explicit alternatives, staged evidence, affordability, procurement, and delivery logic | use as bureaucracy for a small reversible spend or backfill justification for a fixed option |
| Incremental Discounted Cash Flow — DCF with NPV | valuation/appraisal method; incremental cash flows, terminal assumptions, discounting, and NPV | alternatives create attributable cash-flow differences over time | mix accounting profit with cash flow, include sunk costs, fabricate terminal value, or use one discount rate across risks/currencies |
| Internal Rate of Return — IRR | derived return metric | communicate a conventional project's break-even discount rate alongside NPV | rank mutually exclusive projects, nonconventional cash flows produce multiple IRRs, or scale/timing differs materially |
| Total Cost of Ownership — TCO / Life Cycle Costing | lifecycle cost method | acquisition choices move costs into integration, operation, maintenance, risk, transition, and exit | revenue/value or material externalities are silently treated as included |
| Unit economics and contribution model | economic model; price, variable cost, contribution, acquisition/retention, capacity, and cohort logic | test whether each transaction/customer/site creates sustainable contribution before scaling | average margin hides channel, route, returns, utilization, working capital, or fixed-step costs |
| Cost-estimate classification + Basis of Estimate — BoE | estimating framework/artifact; scope, maturity, base date, methods, sources, exclusions, contingency, and range | CAPEX/OPEX estimates must be interpreted according to design definition and decision gate | return a precise point estimate without class, evidence, exclusions, currency/date, and uncertainty |
| Scenario and sensitivity analysis | robustness techniques | determine which market, technical, financial, and policy assumptions reverse a decision | show optimistic/base/pessimistic theater without coherent drivers or interactions |
| Monte Carlo simulation | uncertainty propagation method | defensible distributions, correlations, and model structure can expose outcome ranges and tail risk | historical fit is weak, structural uncertainty dominates, or random output lends false credibility |
| Real Options Analysis | staged-investment method | defer, pilot, expand, switch, abandon, or learn options are actually exercisable and valuable | label every flexible narrative an option without trigger, authority, cost, exclusivity, or competitive consequence |
| Value of Information — VOI | research-decision valuation | decide whether a test, pilot, diligence, or data purchase can change action enough to justify cost | information cannot alter the decision or probabilities/utilities are unsupported |
| Project finance / debt-service model | financing methodology | repayment depends primarily on project cash flows, contracts, covenants, reserves, and risk allocation | sponsor balance-sheet finance or early concept lacks bankable contracts and diligence |
| Comparable-company / transaction valuation | market valuation technique | credible peers and normalized economics can triangulate enterprise/equity value | cherry-pick multiples, ignore capital structure/growth/accounting differences, or value a pre-revenue process solely by mature peers |
| Venture / scenario valuation | financing heuristic family | highly uncertain staged ventures require dilution, failure, follow-on, and exit scenarios | one success multiple is presented as intrinsic value or expected value hides financing path and survival risk |
| Earned Value Management — EVM | delivery-control method | stable baselines and objective progress rules integrate scope, schedule, and cost | use as investment valuation, benefits evidence, or percent-complete theater in discovery work |
| Benefits realization / ex post evaluation | governance and evaluation method | delivered outputs must trace to owner, adoption, operating outcomes, and the original counterfactual | close the case at project delivery or attribute every observed improvement to the investment |
| Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) | economic appraisal method; discounted social costs, benefits, risks, and net value | options have monetizable or explicitly valued social consequences and a common appraisal horizon | force incomparable rights, ecological thresholds, or distributional harms into one price without a stated constraint |
| Social Return on Investment (SROI) | social-value accounting/appraisal method | people affected, material outcomes, deadweight, attribution, displacement, and valuation need an explicit impact account | present a ratio as causal proof, monetize every outcome, or hide negative/distributed effects |
| Distributional analysis | equity-aware appraisal method | costs, benefits, risks, and access differ materially by group, income, geography, or vulnerability | report one aggregate NPV while decision legitimacy depends on who gains and loses |
| Place-based analysis | spatial appraisal specialization | impacts, constraints, services, and opportunity costs vary by place and local system | treat a national average as valid for every locality or double-count spatial benefits |

Core rules: use incremental after-tax cash flows in a consistent nominal/real currency basis; report NPV as primary for value creation; treat IRR/payback as complements; separate unlevered project economics from financing and equity returns; reconcile model totals to source evidence; expose working capital, replacement CAPEX, ramp-up, downtime, salvage, taxes, incentives, and exit.

## Market and commercial intelligence

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intelligence cycle + Key Intelligence Questions — KIQs | decision-intelligence backbone; decision, questions, collection plan, analysis, dissemination, feedback | executives need time-bounded evidence about markets, competitors, customers, or signals | produce reports without a decision, priority, indicator, owner, or update trigger |
| Primary market research | survey/interview/observation experiment family | customer behavior, needs, willingness, usage, awareness, or barriers are not observable in existing data | convenience samples, leading questions, stated intent, and anecdotes are generalized to a market |
| Market sizing — TAM/SAM/SOM | estimation framework | communicate total theoretical demand, serviceable scope, and realistically obtainable demand with explicit constraints | percentages of broad analyst totals replace bottom-up units, price, eligibility, adoption, capacity, and competition |
| Bottom-up demand model | evidence model; entities × incidence/volume × adoption × price/throughput | transaction or source units can be counted and triangulated | overlapping populations, stock vs flow, incompatible periods, or physical supply becomes customer demand automatically |
| Demand forecasting | predictive family | repeated historical/commercial signals support holdout validation and scenario updates | use for a new regime without causal/analog evidence or call prediction a market-size proof |
| Industry structure and value-chain analysis | strategic analysis; actors, flows, margins, power, constraints, substitutes, and regulation | understand where value, bottlenecks, dependency, and bargaining power sit | generic Five Forces prose substitutes for transaction, capacity, and actor evidence |
| Competitor / capability profiles | intelligence artifact | compare verified assets, offers, economics, customers, routes, permits, partnerships, and signals | scraped marketing claims are accepted or “few visible players” proves low competition |
| Jobs-to-be-Done / needs segmentation | discovery and segmentation method | demand differs by circumstance, desired progress, barriers, and trade-offs | personas replace observed behavior or every interview becomes a market segment |
| Segmentation and targeting | analytical/strategy method | differentiated needs, economics, reachability, risk, or service justify distinct choices | clustering has no stable business action or sensitive/proxy variables create unfair exclusion |
| Conjoint / discrete-choice experiment | preference/choice method | estimate trade-offs among attributes and price under a defensible hypothetical choice design | rare/novel options, strategic response, poor attribute design, or stated choice is treated as actual adoption |
| Price elasticity / willingness-to-pay research | economic/research family | pricing decisions need behavioral response and value evidence | ask “what would you pay?” alone or estimate elasticity from confounded historical price changes |
| Mystery shopping / channel audit | observational technique | verify actual offer, service, compliance, availability, or commercial journey | deception violates law/ethics or small observations represent the whole channel |
| Win/loss and funnel analysis | commercial learning method | repeated qualified opportunities can reveal stage leakage, objections, segments, and competitive dynamics | sales notes are unbiased ground truth or conversion change establishes causality |
| Analytical challenge / competing hypotheses | assurance method | strategic claims risk confirmation bias, deception, or sparse evidence | numeric hypothesis scores create false objectivity or challenge occurs after commitment |

Every material market estimate should expose: `unit | geography | period | price basis | source populations | formula | overlap adjustment | uncertainty | supply/demand distinction | triangulation | update trigger`.

## Audit, internal controls, and financial investigation

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Risk-based internal audit — IIA | independent assurance/advisory lifecycle | governance, risk, and controls need a prioritized audit universe, engagements, findings, and follow-up | internal audit assumes management ownership or investigates a case that compromises independence |
| COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework | control framework | design/evaluate control environment, risk assessment, activities, information/communication, and monitoring | one checklist or certification is expected, or reasonable assurance is described as certainty |
| Risk and Control Matrix — RCM | representation artifact; objectives, risks, controls, owners, frequency, evidence, tests, and gaps | trace process/control claims into testable design and operation | every documented activity is called a control or one control is self-tested by its owner |
| Walkthrough | audit/assurance technique | trace representative transactions, systems, documents, and handoffs to confirm actual design | one clean example proves population-wide operating effectiveness |
| Design vs operating-effectiveness testing | assurance distinction | determine first whether a control could prevent/detect the risk, then whether it worked over time | test samples before clarifying control objective, population, owner, frequency, and evidence |
| External financial-statement audit — ISA stack | regulated professional assurance methodology | express an opinion on financial statements under applicable audit/ethical standards | conduct operational audit, fraud investigation, valuation, or guarantee absence of fraud |
| Fraud risk assessment | specialist risk method | identify schemes, actors, incentives/pressures, opportunities, rationalizations, controls, and response | a generic fraud triangle score identifies culprits or low detected incidence proves low fraud |
| Hypothesis-led internal / forensic investigation | investigative backbone | alleged misconduct or financial flows require authorized, impartial, legally defensible fact finding | audit sampling alone establishes individual culpability or the investigator starts from one narrative |
| Flow-of-funds analysis | forensic accounting technique | sources, intermediaries, timing, ownership, transactions, and destinations can be reconstructed from records | transfers imply illegal purpose without legal/context evidence or missing records are filled by speculation |
| Link / temporal analysis | investigative representation | people, entities, accounts, documents, events, and transactions need relational/chronological synthesis | proximity or association is treated as coordination or guilt |
| Digital evidence handling — ISO/IEC 27037 interface | evidence-preservation guidance | digital material may face authenticity, integrity, chain-of-custody, privacy, and admissibility scrutiny | analysts alter originals, collect beyond authority, or standard conformance guarantees admissibility |
| Audit/data analytics | assurance technique | full-population rules, anomaly detection, joins, and repeated controls can direct testing | Benford/outlier/model flag is proof of fraud or poor data lineage is ignored |
| Root-cause and remediation validation | improvement/assurance method | findings should change causal conditions and controls, then be retested | close actions on documentation or owner attestation without effectiveness evidence |

Investigation outputs should distinguish `substantiated | not substantiated | inconclusive`, preserve exculpatory and contradictory evidence, record scope/authority and chain of custody, and obtain legal/forensic specialists where privilege, employment, criminal, regulatory, privacy, or admissibility issues arise.

## Procurement, supplier, and technology due diligence

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source-to-exit / Third-Party Risk Management lifecycle | governance backbone | supplier risk must be owned through need, market, selection, contracting, onboarding, monitoring, renewal, and exit | one onboarding questionnaire is called vendor management |
| Kraljic-style supply segmentation | portfolio technique | supply risk and value/profit impact justify differentiated sourcing strategies | quadrants mechanically award suppliers or one-time spend substitutes for criticality/dependency |
| Make–buy–partner analysis | strategic decision method | capability, capacity, control, speed, learning, IP, risk, and economics determine sourcing form | compare salary vs vendor price without TCO, option value, transition, governance, and exit |
| Market engagement / RFI | discovery technique | requirements and supply-market capability are uncertain before competition | tailor a future tender unfairly, disclose privileged information, or let vendors define the problem unchallenged |
| RFP/RFQ with hard gates + MCDA | controlled selection process | mandatory conditions, evidence, technical/commercial criteria, and accountable scoring support award | weights hide veto requirements, precision is fake, or lowest price wins despite lifecycle risk |
| Supplier identity / beneficial ownership and integrity due diligence | diligence method | sanctions, conflicts, bribery, shell entities, adverse history, and control persons affect eligibility/risk | name screening alone resolves entities or absence in databases proves integrity |
| Financial and operational due diligence | evidence-based assessment | solvency, cash, capacity, concentration, quality, labor, continuity, and delivery claims affect reliance | statements without normalization, site/process evidence, stress cases, or forward obligations are accepted |
| Technology due diligence | multidisciplinary assessment | product claims, architecture, data, security, scale, maintainability, roadmap, IP, team, and economics must survive acquisition/investment | feature demo, diagram, code scan, or vendor roadmap is treated as production assurance |
| Quality Attribute Workshop + ATAM-style analysis | architecture diligence methods | stakeholder scenarios and architectural trade-offs must be elicited and challenged | architecture is unavailable/irrelevant or scores replace evidence and load/failure testing |
| Proof of Concept / benchmark protocol | experimental assurance | uncertain critical claims can be tested with representative workload, thresholds, controls, and exit criteria | vendor-controlled happy-path demo or PoC success is generalized to production scale, operations, and migration |
| Cyber/software supply-chain assurance | assurance family; C-SCRM, SSDF, SBOM, VEX, provenance, and attestations | software, cloud, hardware, or dependencies create material lifecycle risk | SBOM, SOC report, SLSA claim, or ISO certificate is treated as a global security pass |
| Contract/control/evidence matrix | governance artifact | residual risks must become measurable obligations, evidence cadence, remedies, change, audit, and notification rights | boilerplate rights cannot be exercised or service credits substitute for remediation |
| Exit and portability rehearsal | operational assurance test | lock-in, data, identity, continuity, escrow, rebuild, transition, or secure disposal are material | an untested export button or escrow deposit is called exit capability |
| Sustainable procurement — ISO 20400 overlay | guidance overlay | material environmental, labor, rights, circularity, or integrity impacts enter value-for-money and lifecycle controls | ESG score/certificate replaces issue-specific diligence or sustainability compensates a legal/rights veto |

## Composition patterns

### Investment or acquisition thesis

`decision and options → market KIQs + bottom-up sizing → technical/operational model → BoE + DCF/NPV/unit economics → scenario/Monte Carlo → regulatory, environmental, and supply-chain gates → supplier/technology diligence → real option/staged pilot → independent challenge → decision record + benefits monitoring`

### Commercial intelligence

`decision/KIQ → source and collection plan → market sizing + primary research or competitive/value-chain method → triangulation → competing hypotheses/challenge → confidence and indicators → decision → update triggers`

### Financial investigation

`authority/scope → allegation-to-hypotheses → preservation/chain of custody → entity resolution → transaction and flow-of-funds reconstruction → link/time analysis → corroboration and alternative explanations → legal/forensic review → findings state → control remediation and retest`

### Critical-supplier due diligence

`criticality/tiering → identity/integrity → financial/operational evidence → technology/architecture/PoC if applicable → cyber/supply-chain attestations → residual-risk decision → contract/evidence/incident obligations → onboarding validation → monitoring → renewal/exit rehearsal`

## Research anchors and status

Status checked 2026-08-12. Financial, tax, accounting, audit, procurement, sanctions, and disclosure rules remain jurisdiction- and purpose-specific.

- [HM Treasury Green Book 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book-appraisal-and-evaluation-in-central-government/the-green-book-2026) is the current UK public-appraisal edition; adapt outside its scope rather than claiming conformity.
- The Green Book 2026 explicitly separates appraisal from evaluation and includes distributional and place-based analysis; use it as an anchor, not a jurisdiction-free rule.
- [Social Value International standards and guidance](https://www.socialvalueint.org/standards-and-guidance) anchor SROI and social-value accounting; report materiality, deadweight, attribution, displacement, transparency, and verification rather than treating an SROI ratio as causal proof.
- [GAO Cost Estimating and Assessment Guide](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-195g) and live [AACE Recommended Practices](https://web.aacei.org/resources/recommended-practices) are cost-estimate and assurance anchors; estimate classes and ranges depend on sector/scope.
- [ISO 21508:2026](https://www.iso.org/standard/87899.html) is the current EVM guidance; it controls delivery, not investment value.
- [ISO 20252:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/73671.html) remains the published market/opinion/social-research service standard while revision is underway; [ICC/ESOMAR Code 2025](https://standards.esomar.org/assets/documents/icc-esomar-code-2025.pdf) is self-regulatory ethics/transparency guidance.
- [IIA Global Internal Audit Standards](https://www.theiia.org/en/standards/2024-standards/global-internal-audit-standards/) have been effective since 2025-01-09; [COSO Internal Control](https://www.coso.org/guidance-on-ic) remains the 2013 framework.
- ISA 240 (Revised) was issued in 2025 but is effective for periods beginning on or after 2026-12-15; do not present it as already effective at the check date.
- ISO/TS 37008:2023 provides internal-investigation guidance; ISO/IEC 27037:2012 remains an evidence-handling anchor subject to local law.
- [NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 Update 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/upd1/final) is final C-SCRM guidance; NIST SP 1326 became final on 2026-07-08 for ICT supplier due diligence.
- NIST SSDF 1.1 remains final; SSDF 1.2 was draft. [SLSA 1.2](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/) and [CycloneDX 1.7](https://cyclonedx.org/docs/latest) are current specifications, not universal certifications of secure software.
- [ISO 20400:2017](https://www.iso.org/standard/63026.html) remains current sustainable-procurement guidance and is not certifiable.
