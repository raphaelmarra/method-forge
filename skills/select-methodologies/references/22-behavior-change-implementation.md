# Behavior change, implementation, adoption, and scale

Use this catalog when the main uncertainty is why people perform a behavior, how to design an intervention, or how an evidence-supported practice will be adopted, delivered, adapted, scaled, and sustained. Separate intervention efficacy from implementation success.

## Behavioral diagnosis

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Behavioral specification | framing artifact; actor, action, context, time, frequency, and quality | vague goals such as “engage” or “adhere” must become observable | attitude, outcome, or system condition is mislabeled as one behavior |
| COM-B | behavioral diagnostic model; capability, opportunity, motivation | identify broad conditions that must change for a specified behavior | categories are assigned from intuition without evidence or intervention is selected first |
| Theoretical Domains Framework — TDF | determinant framework | a richer theory-informed diagnosis is needed across knowledge, skills, roles, beliefs, context, and regulation | exhaustive coding adds burden without changing intervention choice |
| Capability–opportunity–motivation system map | behavioral/system representation | reciprocal effects and feedback make one-factor explanations weak | diagram is treated as causal proof or population heterogeneity disappears |
| Behavioral journey / friction audit | contextual observation method | steps, defaults, effort, timing, handoffs, and failure points shape action | “friction” language hides coercion, access barriers, or structural constraints |
| Functional behavior assessment | antecedent–behavior–consequence method | recurring behavior may be maintained by identifiable functions and consequences | remote speculation replaces qualified observation, especially in clinical/educational settings |
| EAST | lightweight behavioral design heuristic; easy, attractive, social, timely | low-risk public/service interventions need rapid option generation | mnemonic substitutes for diagnosis, ethics, testing, or structural change |
| MINDSPACE | behavioral-policy checklist | messenger, incentives, norms, defaults, salience, priming, affect, commitments, and ego need coverage | covert influence or checklist completion replaces consent and evidence |
| Fogg Behavior Model | proprietary/simple diagnostic lens; motivation, ability, prompt | a narrow digital/product behavior needs lightweight ideation | complex social/structural behavior is reduced to motivation and prompts |
| Social norms diagnosis | research method | descriptive or injunctive norms may drive behavior | correcting misperceptions could normalize harmful behavior or reference group is wrong |

## Intervention design

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Behaviour Change Wheel — BCW | intervention-design backbone | COM-B diagnosis should map to intervention functions and policy categories | wheel is used without behavioral evidence, context, or later evaluation |
| Behaviour Change Technique Taxonomy v1 — BCTTv1 | intervention-content taxonomy; 93 techniques | specify active ingredients consistently for design, replication, and synthesis | techniques are selected by popularity or taxonomy membership implies efficacy |
| APEASE | option-screening criteria; acceptability, practicability, effectiveness/cost-effectiveness, affordability, spillovers, equity | choose among intervention functions, policies, or techniques | ratings are unsubstantiated or legality/safety is averaged away |
| Intervention Mapping | systematic intervention-development protocol | health/social programs need needs assessment, objectives, theory, components, adoption, and evaluation planning | low-risk simple intervention cannot support the extensive process |
| Behaviour-centred design | design/research family | behavior, environment, emotion, and iterative creative testing must connect | branding replaces behavioral diagnosis and field testing |
| Nudge / choice architecture | intervention family | defaults, ordering, salience, or simplification can improve voluntary choices | coercion, deception, material deprivation, or structural barriers dominate |
| Boosting | capability-building intervention family | durable competence and agency are preferred to steering one choice | users cannot acquire the capability in time or system barriers remain |
| Implementation intentions | self-regulation technique; if–then plan | intention exists but cue-linked enactment is unreliable | motivation, resources, opportunity, or safety is missing |
| Motivational interviewing | collaborative counseling method | ambivalence about change can be explored by trained practitioners | persuasion script, coercive context, or untrained high-stakes clinical use |
| Social marketing | population intervention-planning family | voluntary behavior change needs audience insight, exchange, competition, and coordinated mix | promotion alone is called social marketing or structural/public-policy action is avoided |
| Co-design of interventions | participatory design method | affected people and implementers should shape acceptability and fit | participation is tokenized or transfers expert/safety decisions to participants |
| Default + active-choice design | choice-architecture variants | enrollment/action can be simplified while preserving meaningful choice | stakes, preference heterogeneity, consent, or switching cost make defaults manipulative |

## Implementation determinants and strategies

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Updated CFIR | determinant framework; innovation, outer/inner settings, individuals, and implementation process | systematically identify multilevel barriers and facilitators | every construct is measured regardless of relevance or framework predicts success automatically |
| Normalization Process Theory — NPT | implementation theory | understand sense-making, participation, operational work, and appraisal needed for routine embedding | determinants outside collective implementation work dominate |
| EPIS | phase/context framework; exploration, preparation, implementation, sustainment | implementation crosses outer and inner service-system contexts over time | phases are treated as rigid or intervention development is the real task |
| PARIHS / i-PARIHS | facilitation-oriented framework; innovation/evidence, recipients, context, facilitation | skilled facilitation is a central implementation mechanism | facilitator role, authority, and capacity do not exist |
| Theoretical Domains Framework for implementation | determinant diagnosis | implementer behavior, not recipient behavior, blocks delivery | organizational financing, policy, or infrastructure is the decisive barrier |
| ERIC implementation strategies | taxonomy of implementation strategies | barriers need explicitly named, reproducible strategies | laundry-list selection without mechanism, actor, dose, target, or justification |
| Implementation strategy specification | reporting artifact; actor, action, target, temporality, dose, outcome, justification | a strategy such as “training” or “engagement” is too vague to reproduce | documentation effort exceeds decision value or no one owns delivery |
| Facilitation | implementation strategy | teams need problem solving, relationship building, and adaptation support | facilitator substitutes for leadership, resources, or technical competence |
| Audit and feedback | implementation strategy | performance can be measured repeatedly and credible feedback can change delivery | measures lack validity, feedback is delayed/punitive, or recipients cannot act |
| Educational outreach / academic detailing | targeted implementation strategy | practitioner behavior may change through credible interactive education | one-way information addresses barriers that are actually workflow/incentive based |
| Learning collaborative | multi-site learning strategy | sites can share adaptations, data, and problem solving | meetings replace local action, comparisons are invalid, or participation burden dominates |
| Implementation blueprint | planning artifact; determinants, strategies, owners, sequence, resources, measures | multi-component rollout requires traceability | static plan cannot adapt or duplicates project management without implementation logic |

## Adoption, adaptation, scale, and sustainment

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diffusion of Innovations | adoption theory/lens | relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability, and social system shape spread | adopter labels stereotype people or diffusion is treated as automatic and universally beneficial |
| Technology Acceptance Model / UTAUT | acceptance models | perceived usefulness, ease, social influence, and facilitating conditions support a bounded technology study | survey intention is treated as real use, value, safety, or sustained adoption |
| ADKAR | individual change diagnostic | awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, or reinforcement gaps need practical action | organizational structure/power/incentives are the real mechanism; canonical change card in `02` |
| FRAME | adaptation-reporting framework | modifications to an intervention must be documented by what, why, when, who, and fidelity implications | every local improvisation is beneficial or documentation replaces evaluation |
| Core functions versus forms | adaptation principle/artifact | local delivery must change while preserving causal purpose | “core” components lack evidence or fidelity is enforced to historical form |
| Plan–Do–Study–Act for implementation | iterative adaptation loop | small, observable, safe changes can improve local delivery | uncontrolled cycles are called causal evidence or system-level constraints remain |
| Scale-up pathway / staged rollout | expansion method | evidence, capacity, cost, context, and risk justify progressive replication | pilot success is extrapolated without representativeness, infrastructure, or unit economics |
| Replicating Effective Programs — REP | implementation/replication approach | evidence-based interventions need packaging, training, technical assistance, and local tailoring | evidence base or core components are unclear |
| Sustainability planning | governance method | financing, ownership, workforce, infrastructure, fit, and monitoring must persist | sustainment is assumed after launch or preserves ineffective intervention |
| De-implementation | reduction/removal method | low-value, harmful, obsolete, or unsupported practice should stop | replacement, dependencies, equity, and rebound effects are ignored |

## Evaluation and assurance

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RE-AIM | planning/evaluation framework; reach, effectiveness, adoption, implementation, maintenance | external validity and real-world public impact matter | dimensions are collapsed into one score or causal effect is inferred from implementation metrics |
| PRISM | context-expanded implementation/sustainability framework | multilevel context must be connected to RE-AIM outcomes across phases | all constructs are collected without prioritization or adaptation plan |
| Proctor implementation outcomes | outcome taxonomy | acceptability, adoption, appropriateness, feasibility, fidelity, cost, penetration, and sustainability need separation | outcomes are used without level, timing, measure, or service/clinical outcomes |
| Process evaluation | mixed-method evaluation | reach, dose, fidelity, adaptation, mechanism, and context explain delivery | strong implementation is claimed to prove impact; see `13`/`08` |
| Hybrid effectiveness–implementation designs | study-design family; Types 1–3 | effectiveness and implementation questions should be studied together with declared emphasis | design label obscures underpowered or incompatible objectives |
| Stepped-wedge / phased rollout evaluation | causal/implementation design | sequential rollout is operationally necessary and timing supports inference | secular trends, anticipation, few clusters, or irreversible contamination invalidate estimates |
| Fidelity–adaptation assessment | assurance method | protect core functions while learning local fit | fidelity score rewards paperwork or adaptations are hidden |
| Equity-focused implementation assessment | assurance lens | reach, burden, benefit, adoption, and sustainment may differ across groups/settings | average success masks exclusion or equity is added after deployment |
| Adverse-effect and spillover monitoring | safety/ethics gate | intervention may shift burden, stigmatize, displace, or create unintended behavior | only intended target metric is observed |

## Composition patterns

### Design and test a behavior-change intervention

`specify behavior/population/context → gather behavioral evidence → COM-B/TDF diagnosis → BCW intervention functions → candidate BCTs → APEASE + legal/ethical gates → co-design/prototype → causal + process evaluation → adverse/equity monitoring`

### Implement an evidence-supported practice

`evidence/core functions → CFIR/NPT context diagnosis → prioritized barriers/mechanisms → specified ERIC strategies → implementation blueprint → staged delivery + audit/feedback → RE-AIM/Proctor outcomes → FRAME adaptations → sustain/de-implement decision`

### Scale responsibly

`effectiveness + implementation evidence → representativeness/context comparison → capacity and economics → core-functions/forms → staged rollout → hybrid/process evaluation → infrastructure and financing → maintenance triggers`

## Research anchors and status

Status checked 2026-08-13. Behavioral and implementation frameworks do not authorize manipulation, clinical treatment, or public-policy coercion; verify ethics, jurisdiction, competence, and population.

- [UCL Centre for Behaviour Change](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/brain-sciences/behaviour-change/resources/advancing-behavioural-science) anchors COM-B, BCW, BCTTv1, and APEASE. BCTTv1 contains 93 techniques; membership is not evidence of effectiveness.
- [Updated CFIR](https://cfirguide.org/constructs) is a determinant framework, not an implementation strategy list or outcome model.
- [RE-AIM and PRISM](https://re-aim.org/) support planning, iterative adaptation, evaluation, and sustainment across settings.
- Use original peer-reviewed sources for TDF, NPT, EPIS, i-PARIHS, ERIC, Proctor outcomes, FRAME, and hybrid designs; verify measures and adaptations for the target population.
- Route organizational change to `02`, causal designs to `08`, participatory process design to `24`, education to `18`, and law/ethics to `13`.
