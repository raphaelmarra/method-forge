# Futures, foresight, and strategic anticipation

Use this catalog when uncertainty about the external environment, long time horizons, structural change, or multiple plausible futures could alter a present decision. Foresight explores possibilities and robust choices; it does not predict one future.

## Framing and scanning

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Foresight scoping question | framing artifact; focal issue, horizon, geography, system boundary, audience, and decision | a broad “future of X” inquiry must become decision-relevant | horizon or decision owner is absent and any trend can enter |
| Seven Questions | structured strategic-interview method | tacit expectations, critical issues, preferred outcomes, and wild cards must be elicited quickly | interviews are treated as representative evidence or independent scanning |
| Horizon scanning | continuous evidence method; signals, trends, implications, provenance, and update cadence | emerging changes may create opportunities, threats, or research priorities | news clipping without scope, diversity, source quality, synthesis, or action owner |
| Weak-signal scanning | exploratory detection technique | low-salience early changes could become strategically important | novelty alone is importance or isolated anecdotes are called trends |
| Emerging-issue analysis | assessment method; novelty, plausibility, impact, timing, and indicators | a signal needs structured escalation or dismissal | precision exceeds sparse evidence or hype replaces mechanism |
| Trend / megatrend analysis | longitudinal synthesis | persistent large-scale change across systems should inform assumptions | extrapolate indefinitely, ignore reversals, interactions, or local variation |
| STEEP/PESTLE scanning | coverage framework | scanning needs balanced social, technological, economic, environmental, political, and legal lenses | category completion substitutes for evidence and cross-impact analysis |
| Delphi method | iterative anonymous expert elicitation | dispersed expertise and uncertainty justify controlled feedback across rounds | consensus is treated as truth, panel diversity is weak, or urgent decisions cannot wait |
| Environmental scanning system | organizational capability; sources, scouts, taxonomy, review, repository, escalation | foresight must persist beyond one workshop | central team collects signals that decision makers never review |

## Structuring change and uncertainty

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Driver mapping | synthesis method; drivers ranked by importance and uncertainty | scanning outputs need prioritization and relationships | voting popularity substitutes for evidence, direction, or dependency |
| Cross-impact analysis | interaction method/matrix | drivers may reinforce, inhibit, or make combinations inconsistent | pairwise judgments are arbitrary or matrix size hides reasoning |
| Futures wheel | consequence-mapping technique | first-, second-, and further-order implications need exploration | branches are mistaken for causal proof or probabilities |
| Causal layered analysis — CLA | critical futures method; litany, systemic causes, worldview, and metaphor | dominant narratives and deep assumptions constrain imagined futures | symbolic interpretation substitutes for material evidence and feasible action |
| Three Horizons | transition sense-making framework | incumbent pattern, emerging alternatives, and transition activity must be discussed together | horizons are fixed calendar periods or all novelty belongs in Horizon 3 |
| Morphological analysis | structured possibility-space method | several dimensions and configurations define alternative futures | dependencies make combinations incoherent and no consistency screen follows |
| Uncertainty–impact matrix | prioritization heuristic | distinguish predetermined elements, critical uncertainties, and watch items | one workshop score creates false precision or ignores asymmetric impacts |
| Trend impact analysis | adjusted extrapolation method | a baseline projection can be stress-tested by specified events | historical trend is unstable or event effects are guessed without ranges |
| Cross-impact balance analysis | consistency-analysis family | internally coherent configurations must be distinguished from arbitrary combinations | elicitation quality is weak or model opacity exceeds decision value |
| System dynamics for futures | simulation methodology | feedback, stocks, flows, delays, and policy resistance shape long-term behavior | uncalibrated causal loops are presented as forecasts; canonical modeling in `02`/`08` |

## Building and experiencing futures

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scenario planning | foresight backbone; multiple plausible contextual futures | critical uncertainties could reverse strategic choices | best/base/worst forecasts are relabeled scenarios or one preferred future is hidden as truth |
| 2×2 scenario matrix | lightweight scenario construction | two relatively independent critical uncertainties organize a useful possibility space | more than two drivers are essential or selected axes predetermine a convenient story |
| Intuitive logics | qualitative scenario method | causal narratives and decision implications matter more than quantified probabilities | stories lack internal consistency, evidence, or challenge |
| Probabilistic scenarios | quantified scenario family | explicit events, dependencies, and probability ranges support a decision model | sparse judgments create spurious precision or tail possibilities disappear |
| Archetype scenarios | scenario-generation pattern; continuation, collapse, discipline, transformation | teams need broader configurations than one extrapolation | archetypes force evidence into predetermined stories |
| Visioning | preferred-future method | stakeholders need a concrete normative destination before pathways | vision is confused with plausible forecast or excludes affected groups |
| Normative scenarios | desired/undesired future construction | values and outcomes should be explicit | normative preference is presented as evidence of attainability |
| Experiential futures / design fiction | embodied communication and probe | abstract futures need tangible artifacts to expose assumptions and reactions | artifact is called demand validation, forecast, or implementation proof |
| Speculative design | critical/provocative practice | implications, ethics, and alternatives need debate | provocation replaces decision analysis; execution ownership in `20` |
| Wind tunneling / policy stress-testing | scenario-based evaluation | options must be tested across distinct future conditions | scenarios are redundant or scoring hides fatal conditions |

## Testing and acting

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Robust decision making — RDM | exploratory modeling/decision method | deep uncertainty and many plausible parameter sets make optimal prediction brittle | model cannot represent material uncertainties or computation obscures assumptions |
| Dynamic adaptive policy pathways — DAPP | adaptive pathway method; actions, thresholds, transfer points | decisions can be staged and changed as conditions evolve | actions are irreversible before signals arrive or monitoring cannot detect triggers |
| Assumption-based planning | adaptive planning method | plans depend on load-bearing assumptions that can be monitored | assumptions remain vague, unowned, or without signposts and actions |
| Backcasting | normative planning method | a desired future and time horizon are sufficiently clear to work backward | goal is contested, infeasible, or treated as inevitable |
| Roadmapping | temporal alignment artifact; needs, capabilities, technologies, products, milestones | multiple layers must align toward strategic intent | feature-date promise replaces uncertainty, alternatives, and review |
| Technology roadmapping | R&D/strategy specialization | market needs, product functions, and technology development must align | technical possibility is mistaken for commercial demand; innovation ownership in `28` |
| Real-options staging | investment/decision pattern | learning can be bought through reversible stages before commitment | option value is invoked without real deferral, expansion, abandonment, or evidence plan |
| No-regrets / low-regrets actions | robustness heuristic | some actions create value across most plausible futures | “no regrets” ignores opportunity cost, distribution, lock-in, and scale |
| Contingency planning | branch/action plan | identifiable future conditions require prepared responses | every scenario gets a detailed plan with no trigger or resource feasibility |
| Early-warning indicators / signposts | monitoring artifact | scenario evolution and assumptions can be observed over time | indicators are lagging, noisy, unowned, or never linked to decisions |

## Assurance and measurement

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scenario consistency check | assurance method | narratives must agree with drivers, constraints, chronology, and actor behavior | consensus among authors is the only test |
| Diversity / inclusion audit | assurance gate | futures may omit low-power, affected, regional, or non-dominant perspectives | headcount diversity substitutes for influence and boundary critique |
| Premortem across futures | adversarial technique | expose how a strategy fails differently in each scenario | generic risk list repeats existing registers without future conditions |
| Foresight-to-decision traceability | governance artifact | recommendations must show which signals/scenarios/assumptions support them | workshop outputs cannot be traced to a decision, owner, or trigger |
| Foresight maturity assessment | capability diagnostic | recurring scanning, participation, integration, and learning need improvement | maturity level becomes prestige score or mandates one operating model |
| Signal-to-action metrics | measurement family; coverage, diversity, aging, escalations, decisions changed, trigger latency | operating a continuous foresight capability | count signals or workshops without decision usefulness and false-alarm cost |

## Composition patterns

### Build a strategic foresight cycle

`decision + horizon + boundary → diverse scanning → signal evidence/provenance → driver and cross-impact synthesis → multiple coherent scenarios → option wind-tunnel → robust/adaptive choices → signposts and triggers → periodic refresh`

### Create a normative transition pathway

`affected-stakeholder frame → present-system evidence → vision and constraints → backcasting → capability/technology roadmaps → staged options → milestones + early warnings → reassessment`

### Evaluate a strategy under deep uncertainty

`load-bearing assumptions → scenario ensemble / parameter ranges → failure and regret criteria → RDM or DAPP → near-term actions → adaptation pathways → monitoring triggers`

## Research anchors and status

Status checked 2026-08-13. Foresight methods are often facilitation families rather than predictive models; verify the exact variant and evidence behind inputs.

- [UK Government Office for Science Futures Toolkit 2024](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts/the-futures-toolkit-html) defines 12 tools and seven composition pathways, including Delphi, horizon scanning, Three Horizons, scenarios, stress-testing, roadmapping, and backcasting.
- [OECD Strategic Foresight](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/programmes/strategic-foresight.html) anchors horizon scanning, megatrends, scenarios, visioning, and backcasting for decision readiness.
- Use original methodological literature for CLA, morphological analysis, cross-impact balance, RDM, and DAPP; distinguish a named method from a consultant-local workshop variant.
- Route causal models and uncertainty quantification to `08`; strategy to `02`; innovation roadmaps to `28`; experiential/speculative design to `20`.
