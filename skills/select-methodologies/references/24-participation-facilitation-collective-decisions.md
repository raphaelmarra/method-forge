# Participation, facilitation, deliberation, and collective decisions

Use this catalog when affected people, stakeholders, experts, teams, or citizens should inform, shape, monitor, or share a decision. Define the actual influence promised before selecting a format. Participation without decision space or feedback is extractive theater.

## Participation framing and recruitment

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OECD ten-step participation pathway | public-participation backbone | design, implement, use inputs, give feedback, and evaluate a consequential process | no genuine problem or decision space exists for participants to influence |
| IAP2 Spectrum | role/promise framework; inform, consult, involve, collaborate, empower | decision makers must state the public's degree of influence | levels are treated as a maturity ladder or “empower” is promised without authority |
| Stakeholder salience analysis | prioritization lens; power, legitimacy, urgency | engagement resources and risks differ across stakeholders | high power is equated with moral priority or low-power affected groups disappear |
| Affected-rights-interest matrix | inclusion artifact | distinguish decision authority, rights holders, affected groups, expertise, and implementation roles | all stakeholders are treated as equivalent or rights become preferences |
| Participation objective and promise | governance artifact | specify needed input, decision phase, influence, response, owner, and limits | open invitation creates expectations the sponsor cannot honor |
| Participation plan | operating artifact; scope, participants, methods, access, timeline, data, feedback, evaluation | multi-stage engagement needs accountable design | event logistics substitute for decision linkage and inclusion |
| Open recruitment | sampling/recruitment method | broad voluntary participation and transparency are appropriate | self-selection is represented as population opinion |
| Purposive stakeholder recruitment | targeted method | specific experience, expertise, role, or affectedness is required | sponsor hand-picks agreeable voices or omits critics |
| Civic lottery / stratified random selection | representative recruitment method | a deliberative mini-public should reflect population diversity | small sample is called statistically representative of every opinion or attrition distorts composition |
| Snowball recruitment | network-reach method | hidden or hard-to-reach communities require trusted referrals | network homophily and gatekeepers bias participation |
| Accessibility and participation accommodation plan | inclusion artifact | disability, language, time, care, travel, technology, safety, or compensation affect access | accessibility is added after format, venue, and tools are fixed |

## Participation and deliberation methods

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public consultation | structured input method | a proposal or options can materially change in response to submissions | decision is already fixed, response criteria are absent, or volume is treated as a vote |
| Open meeting / town hall | public dialogue format | visibility, broad access, questions, and direct exchange are valuable | dominant speakers, unsafe conflict, or complex deliberation requires smaller groups |
| Focus group | qualitative group research | language, norms, reactions, and diversity of views need exploration | representativeness, voting, usability, or consensus is claimed; research owner in `31`/`20` |
| World Café | rotating small-group dialogue method | many participants should connect perspectives around focused questions | outputs need traceable individual positions or high conflict requires tighter structure |
| Open Space Technology | participant-led agenda method | motivated participants can self-organize around complex issues | mandatory coverage, formal decisions, or low psychological safety require designed agenda |
| Future Search | whole-system planning conference | diverse system actors can build shared past/present/future understanding and common-ground action | sponsor cannot support multi-day preparation, representation, and follow-through |
| Appreciative Inquiry summit | strengths-oriented collective-change method | generative stories and desired futures can mobilize action | harm, failure, power, or accountability must be confronted directly |
| Charrette | intensive collaborative design process | stakeholders and technical teams need iterative place/service/design development | compressed event bypasses evidence, affected groups, or statutory process |
| Citizen assembly | representative deliberative process | complex, value-laden, long-term public questions need informed collective recommendations | authority, balanced evidence, random selection, time, independence, and official response are absent |
| Citizen jury / panel | smaller deliberative mini-public | a bounded question can be examined through evidence and facilitated deliberation | few days cannot address the scope or sponsor cherry-picks evidence/witnesses |
| Consensus conference | public-expert deliberation method | non-experts should question experts and develop considered conclusions | technical uncertainty is hidden or consensus is forced |
| Participatory budgeting | resource-allocation process | participants can propose, deliberate, and decide or advise on a defined budget | budget is trivial, eligibility opaque, implementation uncertain, or popularity overrides feasibility/equity |
| Participatory action research | inquiry/action methodology | participants should co-own questions, evidence, action, and learning | research rigor, role conflicts, safety, and ownership are unmanaged |
| Citizen science | participation/research model | communities can shape or contribute observations, analysis, and action | unpaid data extraction, unsafe tasks, biased coverage, or no data/benefit governance |
| Civic monitoring / social audit | accountability method | communities can observe commitments, services, budgets, or outcomes and obtain response | monitoring lacks access, protection, verification, or remedy |
| Crowdsourcing / public challenge | open contribution method | broad idea, evidence, or solution search benefits from diverse contributors | participation terms, IP, bias, feasibility, and selection criteria are unclear |

## Facilitation and group sense-making

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Facilitated workshop design | process backbone; purpose, participants, sequence, activities, outputs, decisions | synchronous interaction adds value beyond independent work | meeting is used for information transfer or sponsor lacks action authority |
| ORID focused conversation | facilitation sequence; objective, reflective, interpretive, decisional | groups need to move from facts through meaning to action | emotionally unsafe or complex evidence needs prior analysis |
| Nominal Group Technique — NGT | structured idea/prioritization method | equal initial contribution and transparent ranking can limit dominance | ranked preferences are mistaken for evidence or consensus |
| Brainwriting / 6-3-5 | parallel ideation technique | reduce production blocking and speaking dominance | problem framing/evidence is missing or idea volume is the objective |
| Affinity mapping | synthesis technique | many qualitative observations or ideas need bottom-up clustering | facilitator-imposed clusters hide disagreement or frequency equals importance |
| Dialogue mapping / IBIS | issue-based representation | questions, ideas, arguments, and decisions need live traceability | notation overhead disrupts sensitive dialogue or map is mistaken for truth |
| Liberating Structures | facilitation pattern library | groups need distributed participation with adaptable microstructures | fashionable activity selection lacks purpose, psychological safety, and follow-through |
| Fishbowl | dialogue format | a large group should observe rotating focused discussion | center/periphery design reinforces status or topic is unsafe for public performance |
| Circle process | relational dialogue format | equal voice, listening, and shared norms support a sensitive conversation | urgent decision, investigation, or severe power/safety issue requires formal process |
| Graphic facilitation | visual sense-making technique | complex discussion benefits from shared visible synthesis | aesthetics create false consensus or inaccessible visuals exclude participants |
| Facilitation ground rules / working agreement | governance artifact | interaction norms, confidentiality, challenge, turn-taking, and escalation need clarity | rules suppress dissent or promise confidentiality that cannot be guaranteed |

## Collective prioritization and decision

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dot voting | lightweight prioritization heuristic | quickly surface preferences after shared understanding | vote count substitutes for evidence, feasibility, rights, or minority impact |
| Multi-voting | iterative narrowing technique | a large option set needs transparent reduction | sequential voting amplifies popularity and correlated preferences |
| Consent decision making | governance method; proceed absent reasoned paramount objection | groups need safe-to-try decisions and explicit objections | “no objection” is coerced, authority unclear, or irreversible stakes demand stronger approval |
| Consensus decision making | agreement process | implementation depends on broad ownership and time allows integration of concerns | unanimity enables veto, silence equals consent, or legal authority belongs elsewhere |
| Advice process | distributed-decision pattern | empowered individuals can decide after consulting expertise and affected people | decision rights, accountability, and required consultation are vague |
| Sociocracy consent and circles | governance system | semi-autonomous groups need linked domains, consent, and feedback | wholesale governance adoption exceeds problem or formal accountability conflicts |
| Delphi | asynchronous expert aggregation | anonymity and iteration reduce status pressure for uncertain expert judgments | public values/legitimacy or factual truth is the question; canonical owner in `31` |
| MCDA in groups | formal collective-decision method | alternatives and criteria require explicit value trade-offs and sensitivity | aggregation hides value conflict or weights create false objectivity; canonical owner in `08` |
| Gradients of Agreement | preference-expression scale | distinguish endorsement, reservations, abstention, and blocking | scale replaces substantive reasoning and decision rule |
| Minority report / dissent record | assurance artifact | unresolved disagreement and reasons should remain visible | dissent is stigmatized or record exposes vulnerable participants |

## Digital participation and assurance

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Online deliberation | digital process family | geography, time, scale, or accessibility supports moderated asynchronous/synchronous participation | digital divide, harassment, identity, privacy, or platform control is unmanaged |
| Pol.is-style opinion clustering | computational deliberation technique | large-scale statements and agreement patterns can reveal common ground and divisions | algorithm/output opacity, brigading, representativeness, or nuance makes clusters misleading |
| Participatory GIS | spatial participation method | local knowledge, place impacts, and geographic priorities inform decisions | location privacy, digital exclusion, projection/scale, or map authority is unmanaged |
| Balanced information pack | deliberative assurance artifact | participants need accessible, accurate, plural evidence before judgment | sponsor controls framing, omissions, experts, and uncertainty |
| Independent facilitation / oversight | integrity control | sponsor incentives or high stakes threaten neutrality and trust | independence is nominal or third party lacks authority/resources |
| Participation evaluation | assurance framework; inclusion, process, influence, experience, decision quality, follow-through | demonstrate quality and learn across processes | satisfaction or attendance alone proves legitimacy and impact |
| Feedback-loop closure | accountability method | participants must learn how input was considered, adopted, rejected, or deferred | generic thank-you obscures decisions and reasons |
| Participation data governance | privacy/ethics artifact | identities, recordings, submissions, locations, and vulnerable-group data are collected | consent is bundled, retention indefinite, public disclosure unsafe, or vendor reuse unclear |

## Composition patterns

### Design a legitimate participation process

`decision space + authority → participation objective/promise → affected-rights-stakeholder map → recruitment + accommodations → balanced information → fit-for-purpose method → facilitated input/deliberation → transparent synthesis → authorized response → feedback closure → independent evaluation`

### Run a representative deliberative process

`bounded public question → independent governance → civic lottery + inclusion supports → balanced evidence/witnesses → learning → facilitated deliberation → recommendations + dissent → public response → implementation tracking`

### Facilitate an internal collective decision

`decision owner/rule → prework/evidence → diverse individual generation → structured sense-making → criteria/trade-offs → consent/consensus/advice decision → dissent and rationale → owners/actions → review trigger`

## Research anchors and status

Status checked 2026-08-13. Participation formats do not themselves confer democratic legitimacy; verify authority, inclusion, representation, accessibility, privacy, and promised influence.

- [OECD Guidelines for Citizen Participation Processes](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-guidelines-for-citizen-participation-processes_f765caf6-en.html) define ten design/implementation steps, eight method families, and quality principles.
- [IAP2](https://www.iap2.org/page/SpectrumEvolution) owns the participation spectrum and is reviewing its evolution in 2026; verify the current adopted version before naming levels or promises.
- Use original sources for NGT, World Café, Open Space, Future Search, citizen assemblies, consent governance, participatory GIS, and computational deliberation.
- Route negotiation/mediation to `23`, research validity to `07`, decision models to `08`, service/design co-creation to `20`, and public-policy authority to `13`.
