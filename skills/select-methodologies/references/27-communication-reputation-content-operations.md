# Communication, reputation, and content operations

Use this catalog when an organization must understand audiences, communicate credibly, govern messages and evidence, operate a multi-channel content lifecycle, manage reputation, or communicate risk and crisis. Distribution is a system of audience, purpose, content, channel, timing, governance, and measurement—not a posting checklist.

## Communication strategy and narrative

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Communication strategy | backbone; outcome, audience, behavior/understanding, message, messenger, channel, timing, resources, measures | coordinated communication must support a defined decision or outcome | activity/calendar is called strategy or communication is used to fix a non-communication problem |
| Audience / stakeholder communication map | segmentation artifact | audiences differ in stakes, knowledge, trust, access, language, behavior, or influence | demographics alone determine needs or high-power groups erase affected groups |
| Communication objective ladder | planning artifact; awareness, understanding, belief, capability, action, relationship | distinguish what communication can plausibly change | reach/engagement is accepted as final outcome or causal sequence is assumed |
| Message house | message-governance artifact; core proposition, pillars, proof, qualifiers | spokespeople and channels need consistent prioritized messages | rigid slogans ignore audience questions, uncertainty, and evidence |
| Message architecture + claim–proof matrix | representation/assurance system | claims, evidence, applicability, wording, owners, and audience variants need control | every benefit is published or supporting evidence is stale/non-generalizable |
| Narrative strategy | meaning/sequence framework | facts require coherent actors, tension, stakes, choices, and desired future | storytelling manipulates, invents causality, or displaces critical facts |
| Framing analysis | research/strategy method | alternative problem definitions, metaphors, values, and omissions shape interpretation | preferred frame is selected without audience/ethical testing or facts are distorted |
| Plain-language process | content method | people must find, understand, and act correctly under real literacy/context | simplification removes necessary nuance, conditions, risk, or legal accuracy |
| Messenger / source strategy | trust and reach method | credibility, relationship, expertise, identity, and access differ by audience | authority is borrowed deceptively or influencer popularity substitutes for competence |
| Communication journey | temporal/channel representation | information needs and actions change before, during, and after a decision/event | generic funnel ignores feedback, offline routes, and exceptional circumstances |
| Strategic communication campaign | coordinated intervention | multiple messages, messengers, channels, and phases support a bounded outcome | campaign substitutes for policy/service/product change or lacks baseline and evaluation |

## Public relations, issues, and reputation

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Issues management | anticipatory process; scan, assess, prioritize, position, act, monitor | emerging social/political/operational issues may affect license, trust, or policy | PR narrative substitutes for correcting harmful conduct |
| Reputation risk assessment | risk method | stakeholder expectations, conduct, visibility, contagion, and response shape material risk | sentiment score is called reputation or reputation management means concealment |
| Stakeholder relationship management | operating model | durable two-way relationships and feedback matter beyond campaigns | stakeholder contact database is called engagement or influence becomes manipulation |
| Media relations plan | relationship/distribution method | journalists need timely access, evidence, spokespeople, context, and responses | coverage volume overrides accuracy, independence, and relevance |
| Press office / newsroom workflow | operational process | inquiries, statements, assets, approvals, corrections, and monitoring need rapid control | centralized approval creates dangerous delay or unverified speed |
| Press briefing / media availability | communication format | material developments require equitable live explanation and questioning | staged event blocks scrutiny, unsupported answers, or confidentiality/safety risk |
| Thought-leadership program | expertise communication system | original evidence and credible expertise can advance understanding/category | ghostwritten opinion, recycled content, or commercial claims pose as independent knowledge |
| Executive positioning | leadership communication strategy | leader voice and visibility support legitimate organizational objectives | personality cult, undisclosed promotion, or leader outruns evidence and operations |
| Community relations / social-license process | relationship/participation method | operations materially affect places and ongoing consent/trust/benefit sharing matter | sponsorship substitutes for impact mitigation, rights, or meaningful participation |
| Public affairs | policy/stakeholder communication discipline | lawful transparent engagement with public institutions and policy debate is required | covert influence, astroturfing, or messaging bypasses compliance and evidence |
| Reputation recovery | remediation communication process | credible acknowledgement, remedy, proof, governance change, and monitoring can rebuild trust | apology/campaign precedes facts, accountability, victim needs, and operational correction |

## Content strategy and editorial operations

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Content strategy | governance method; audience needs, purpose, portfolio, formats, distribution, lifecycle, ownership, measures | content supports discovery, understanding, use, trust, service, or retention over time | publishing volume/trends define success; marketing specialization in `19` |
| Content inventory | census artifact | existing content, location, format, owner, status, audience, and dependencies must be known | inventory completion substitutes for quality and disposition decisions |
| Content audit | evaluative method | accuracy, usefulness, duplication, accessibility, performance, risk, and lifecycle need decisions | traffic alone determines keep/remove or old URLs are changed without migration evidence |
| Content model / structured content | semantic schema | reusable content types, fields, relationships, metadata, and channel outputs need consistency | page layout/CMS limitation defines semantics; design owner in `20` |
| Content lifecycle | governance backbone; propose, create, review, approve, publish, maintain, archive/remove | accuracy, ownership, expiry, and accountability matter across time | workflow is equally heavy for all risk tiers or no owner maintains content |
| Editorial calendar | scheduling artifact | planned themes, formats, dependencies, events, owners, and cadence need coordination | calendar demand creates low-value content or responsiveness disappears |
| Content portfolio / pillar model | strategy representation | themes and audience needs require intentional coverage and investment | pillars are SEO buckets without differentiated user/business role |
| Hub-and-spoke | information/distribution pattern | a substantive hub can support linked specialized content and journeys | thin spokes duplicate content or links are designed only for ranking |
| Content atomization | reuse technique | a validated source asset can yield channel-appropriate components efficiently | context is lost, fragments overclaim, or repetition creates audience fatigue |
| COPE — Create Once, Publish Everywhere | structured-reuse principle | canonical modular content should feed multiple channels consistently | identical presentation ignores channel, audience, accessibility, and context |
| Single source of truth for content | governance/architecture pattern | regulated, technical, product, policy, or service facts must remain consistent | central repository becomes bottleneck or cannot represent variants/status |
| Editorial workflow / content supply chain | operating process | briefs, research, creation, review, assets, localization, publishing, and measurement cross roles/tools | process optimization rewards throughput over usefulness and quality |
| Editorial brief | planning artifact | purpose, audience, question, action, evidence, format, distribution, constraints, and acceptance need clarity | rigid template replaces discovery or commissioned output lacks decision use |
| Editorial board | governance cadence | portfolio choices, standards, risk, resourcing, and performance need cross-functional decisions | committee approves every low-risk item and blocks timely ownership |
| Content governance RACI / decision rights | accountability artifact | creation, factual review, legal approval, publishing, maintenance, and retirement are ambiguous | everyone is consulted or accountability is assigned without capacity |
| Localization workflow | adaptation process | language, culture, law, units, examples, search, and support differ by locale | literal translation is called localization or uncontrolled variants drift |
| Transcreation | creative localization method | emotional, idiomatic, and cultural effect must be recreated | regulated/technical meaning can change or no native review/evidence exists |
| Rights and clearance workflow | legal/operational gate | text, image, music, footage, data, likeness, license, territory, duration, and reuse rights matter | asset availability is assumed to imply permission |
| Content expiry / sunset policy | lifecycle control | time-sensitive, regulated, product, event, or campaign content can become harmful/stale | content persists indefinitely or deletion breaks legal/archive/user needs |

## Distribution and channel orchestration

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PESO model | proprietary channel-classification framework; paid, earned, shared, owned | teams need a simple integrated channel vocabulary and role map | categories are treated as a complete strategy or boundaries/canonical owner are ignored |
| POE media model | channel heuristic; paid, owned, earned | lightweight allocation and integration discussion | social/shared and partner dynamics are material but disappear |
| Channel-role matrix | strategy artifact; discovery, explanation, proof, conversion, service, community, retention | each channel needs a declared audience/job and handoff | platform presence is justified by popularity alone |
| Omnichannel content orchestration | operating/design method | audiences move across owned, social, search, media, partner, email, physical, and assisted channels | visual consistency is called continuity while state, message, consent, and ownership break |
| Syndication | distribution method | trusted partners/platforms can republish controlled content to extend reach | duplicate/outdated copies, rights, canonical links, attribution, and corrections are unmanaged |
| Embargo / timed release | coordination protocol | multiple parties need fair preparation and synchronized publication | embargo is used to suppress legitimate reporting or leaks create unequal access |
| Release train / publishing cadence | operating pattern | recurring multi-channel packages need predictable coordination | cadence delays urgent corrections or creates content regardless of value |
| Always-on distribution | continuous operating model | persistent audience needs/search/community justify ongoing publishing and response | constant presence creates low-quality noise and operational burnout |
| Campaign burst | time-bounded distribution pattern | launch/event/decision has a defined window, sequence, and saturation plan | burst reaches people without follow-up, service capacity, or retention path |
| Creator / influencer collaboration workflow | partnership method | trusted creators can interpret and reach relevant communities | undisclosed sponsorship, brand-safety, rights, factual control, or audience exploitation is unmanaged |
| Employee advocacy | distributed communication program | willing informed staff can credibly share expertise and culture | participation is coerced, private accounts governed, or claims are uncontrolled |
| Community distribution | relationship pattern | peer participation, moderation, and reciprocal value sustain relevance | community is treated as free reach and extractive promotion |
| Distribution checklist / preflight | assurance gate | metadata, links, tracking, accessibility, rights, approvals, rendering, timing, and rollback must pass | checkbox cannot detect wrong strategy, evidence, or harmful message |

## Risk, crisis, and infodemic communication

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crisis and Emergency Risk Communication — CERC | evidence-based crisis communication framework | emergencies require rapid credible empathetic actionable communication across phases | ordinary brand issue is dramatized as emergency or CERC replaces incident command |
| Risk Communication and Community Engagement — RCCE | public-health/emergency method family | risk information and community partnership must jointly shape workable protective action | one-way messaging is called engagement or local context/behavior is ignored |
| Crisis communication plan | preparedness artifact | roles, approvals, channels, audiences, scenarios, templates, contacts, monitoring, and exercises are needed before crisis | static binder lacks activation, alternates, accessibility, and tests |
| Holding statement | rapid interim artifact | verified facts are incomplete but acknowledgement, known/unknown, action, and update time are needed | vague corporate language delays urgent protective action or implies unsupported certainty |
| Message mapping | risk-communication technique | complex/high-stress questions need concise prioritized messages and evidence | formula suppresses uncertainty, empathy, and audience questions |
| Spokesperson protocol and training | capability/control | credible authorized people must communicate under pressure and questioning | scripted polish replaces subject competence and transparency |
| Rumor / misinformation management | listening-response workflow | harmful uncertainty, questions, narratives, and false claims affect behavior | every dissent is labeled misinformation or rebuttal amplifies low-reach claims |
| Social listening with verification | sensing method | emerging questions, harm, confusion, and coordination signals need timely detection | platform sample is population opinion or sentiment tool decides truth |
| Prebunking / inoculation | preventive communication intervention | recurring manipulation techniques can be taught before exposure | broad warnings create cynicism or intervention lacks population testing |
| Correction protocol | error-repair method | published material is materially wrong, misleading, stale, or incomplete | silent overwrite hides history or correction repeats/amplifies harm unnecessarily |
| Crisis dark site / alternate channel | resilience pattern | primary website/channel may fail or demand may surge during emergency | stale duplicate information and unclear authority create conflict |
| Communication exercise / simulation | preparedness test | roles, approvals, monitoring, spokespeople, channels, and update cadence need rehearsal | tabletop discussion lacks injects, evidence, observers, remediation, and retest |

## Measurement and assurance

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AMEC Integrated Evaluation Framework | communication measurement framework | objectives, inputs, activities, outputs, outtakes, outcomes, and impact need traceability | framework completion implies causality or business impact without evidence |
| Barcelona Principles 3.0 | measurement principles | communication evaluation needs outcomes, stakeholders, qualitative/quantitative evidence, integrity, and rejection of AVEs | principles are treated as a measurement method or certification |
| Content performance framework | metric tree | findability, reach, attention, comprehension, action, service, retention, trust, risk, and cost need balanced sensing | views, likes, or output volume become the objective |
| Readability/comprehension testing | content evaluation | critical messages must be understood by representative audiences | formula readability equals comprehension, accessibility, or behavior |
| Message testing | research method | meaning, credibility, relevance, emotion, misunderstanding, and action need pretest | preference winner is assumed to change real-world behavior |
| Distribution incrementality test | causal family | determine audience/outcome caused by channel/distribution intervention | platform attribution or exposed/unexposed self-selection is called causal |
| Reputation tracking | longitudinal mixed-method system | trust, expectations, experience, conduct, narratives, and stakeholder outcomes change over time | one sentiment index or media tone becomes reputation truth |
| Editorial quality review | assurance process | accuracy, evidence, clarity, accessibility, ethics, rights, consistency, and usefulness need independent checks | style/polish substitutes for factual and user validation |
| Correction and freshness metrics | governance sensors | error severity, correction latency, stale exposure, review completion, and recurrence need improvement | low corrections implies quality despite weak detection or suppresses reporting |

## Composition patterns

### Build and distribute a content system

`audience decisions/needs → communication objectives → content audit/portfolio → claim–proof + content model → briefs/editorial workflow → risk-tier review → channel-role/PESO orchestration → accessible publication → outcome/incrementality evidence → maintenance/correction/sunset`

### Communicate during a crisis

`incident truth owner → affected audiences + protective actions → known/unknown/next update → CERC/RCCE messages + trusted messengers → coordinated channels/alternates → questions/rumor sensing → frequent verified updates → corrections → recovery/AAR/retest`

### Recover reputation after harm

`facts + affected parties → accountability and immediate protection → independent investigation where needed → acknowledgement/apology/remedy → operational/governance change → evidence and transparent updates → stakeholder outcome/trust monitoring`

## Research anchors and status

Status checked 2026-08-13. Platform algorithms, formats, analytics, and policies are volatile; verify them at use time. Platform tactics are implementation details, not durable methodologies.

- [CDC CERC Manual](https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/php/cerc-manual/index.html) provides an evidence-based emergency communication framework; chapter revision dates vary.
- [WHO RCCE](https://www.who.int/emergencies/risk-communications) and its 2024 competency framework anchor risk communication and community engagement across emergency phases.
- Use [AMEC](https://amecorg.com/) primary materials for the Integrated Evaluation Framework and Barcelona Principles; principles do not prove causal impact.
- PESO and several content-marketing labels are proprietary/industry frameworks; verify ownership and avoid presenting them as standards.
- Route marketing/content acquisition to `19`, content/IA/accessibility to `20`, participation to `24`, journalism verification to `29`, and crisis governance/law to `13`.
