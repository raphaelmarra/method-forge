# Innovation, R&D, and technology management

Use this catalog when an organization must govern innovation capability, discover and select opportunities, manage ideas and experiments, develop technology, protect/share knowledge, build partnerships, manage portfolios, transfer technology, or commercialize evidence. Innovation management does not replace product discovery, engineering verification, or investment appraisal.

## Innovation system and strategy

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISO 56001:2024 | requirements standard for an innovation management system | organization needs auditable capability, policy, leadership, processes, support, evaluation, and improvement | task-level recipe or certification theater substitutes for innovation outcomes |
| ISO 56002:2019 | guidance framework for innovation management systems | tailor a systematic organization-wide capability without certification requirements | detailed method is expected; second edition draft is presented as stable |
| Innovation strategy | governance artifact; ambition, domains, thesis, portfolio, capabilities, boundaries, metrics | innovation activity must align with strategic intent and resources | slogans such as “disrupt” replace choices, evidence, and stop rules |
| Innovation thesis | selection artifact | define where/why/how the organization expects to create value and learn | thesis is insulated from disconfirming market/technology evidence |
| Innovation ambition matrix | portfolio heuristic; core, adjacent, transformational | balance horizons/types of innovation and resource expectations | arbitrary percentages or labels replace risk, adjacency, and capability analysis |
| Three Horizons for innovation | transition portfolio lens | incumbent improvement, emerging options, and future systems interact | horizons are fixed budgets/timelines or excuse speculative pet projects |
| Open innovation | boundary/partnership paradigm | useful knowledge, technologies, markets, and paths exist outside/inside organization | indiscriminate openness leaks rights, ignores absorption capacity, or replaces internal capability |
| Ambidextrous organization | organization-design approach | exploitation and exploration need differentiated structures and integration | two isolated cultures compete without strategic governance and transfer |
| Innovation ecosystem mapping | actor/capability/value-flow method | universities, startups, suppliers, customers, funders, regulators, and complements shape opportunity | logo map lacks exchanged value, incentives, dependencies, and decision use |
| Strategic intelligence management — ISO 56006:2021 | guidance framework | innovation decisions need governed collection, analysis, sharing, and use of intelligence | competitive surveillance is confused with espionage or data volume replaces decisions |
| Innovation management assessment — ISO/TR 56004:2019 | diagnostic guidance | capability strengths/gaps need structured assessment and improvement priorities | maturity score becomes prestige/certification or dictates one operating model |

## Opportunity, idea, and concept development

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opportunity and idea management — ISO 56007:2023 | guidance framework | opportunities/ideas need systematic identification, evaluation, development, and decision | every suggestion enters bureaucracy or idea count becomes success |
| Opportunity framing | artifact; need, beneficiary, context, mechanism, value, constraints, evidence, unknowns | raw idea must become a testable opportunity | solution remains embedded and customer/system problem is unsupported |
| Front-end of innovation / fuzzy front end | lifecycle family | early opportunity, insight, concept, and feasibility uncertainty need explicit governance | process promises to eliminate ambiguity or delays cheap experiments |
| Jobs-to-be-Done | opportunity-discovery lens | progress, circumstance, barriers, and switching explain demand | interview narratives prove market size; canonical owner in `12`/`19` |
| Lead-user method | innovation research method | users facing advanced needs may reveal solutions before the broader market | unusual users are treated as representative demand or IP/selection is unmanaged |
| Ethnographic / contextual inquiry | field-research method | latent practices, workarounds, constraints, and needs must be observed | observation alone gives prevalence/causality; research owner in `31`/`20` |
| TRIZ | systematic inventive problem-solving family | technical contradictions and known inventive principles can stimulate concepts | jargon/mechanical analogy replaces engineering evidence and usability |
| Morphological analysis | concept-generation method | multiple solution dimensions and combinations need systematic exploration | dependent dimensions produce incoherent option explosion |
| Design Thinking / Double Diamond | human-centered innovation family | desirability and problem/solution uncertainty justify divergent/convergent research and prototyping | generic workshops replace domain, feasibility, economics, and assurance; owner in `20` |
| Design sprint | timeboxed prototype/test method | a bounded high-priority question can be tested rapidly | deep science, regulation, longitudinal adoption, or production readiness is the uncertainty |
| Concept screening / weighted matrix | comparative heuristic | comparable concepts and criteria need transparent narrowing | invented weights/scores create false precision or fatal gates are averaged |
| Concept test | research method | comprehension, relevance, differentiation, credibility, and intent need early evidence | stated preference proves purchase or technical feasibility |
| Pretotype / smoke test | demand experiment | behavior can be tested before building expensive capability | deception, safety, regulation, brand harm, or technical feasibility dominates |

## R&D, experimentation, and technology maturation

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scientific method / hypothesis testing | research backbone | falsifiable explanatory/technical claims require controlled evidence | exploratory discovery is forced into premature hypotheses or null result is suppressed |
| Design of Experiments — DOE | experimental design family | multiple controllable factors/interactions affect response and resources are limited | uncontrolled nuisance factors, wrong experimental unit, or unsafe combinations |
| Sequential experimentation | adaptive research method | each result should update next test while controlling evidence quality | optional stopping/data peeking inflates claims or approvals require fixed protocol |
| Build–Measure–Learn | entrepreneurial learning loop | business/product hypotheses can be tested cheaply and reversibly | unsafe/regulated “MVP” or activity metrics replace falsification |
| Stage-Gate | proprietary development/governance methodology | material projects need staged evidence and resource decisions across development | bureaucracy, gate theater, or certainty is demanded too early |
| Technology Readiness Levels — TRL | maturity scale | evidence of technology maturation must be communicated consistently | TRL alone proves manufacturability, integration, economics, safety, or market readiness |
| Technology Maturity Assessment — TMA | evidence review method | TRL claims need independent requirements, environment, demonstration, and evidence assessment | self-rating or component TRL is generalized to system readiness |
| Advancement Degree of Difficulty — AD2 | maturation-difficulty assessment | understand challenge to advance from current maturity to required level | maturity number is treated as schedule/cost forecast without task evidence |
| Manufacturing Readiness Levels — MRL | production-maturity scale | materials, process, quality, workforce, supply, and capacity must mature with technology | TRL and MRL are conflated or generic scale replaces sector criteria |
| Integration Readiness Levels — IRL | interface/integration maturity heuristic | independently mature technologies may fail at integration | scale is treated as universal validated standard or systems engineering is skipped |
| System Readiness Level — SRL | composite maturity concept | communicate combined technology/integration maturity cautiously | arithmetic combination hides weakest-link evidence and mission context |
| Technology roadmapping | layered planning method | market/mission needs, products/capabilities, technologies, and timing must align | roadmap promises dates or assumes one technology path |
| Demonstration / pilot program | staged evidence method | performance, operability, integration, users, environment, and economics require representative proof | showcase omits baseline, failure, duration, scale differences, and stop criteria |
| Scale-up protocol | engineering/R&D method | transport, kinetics, heat/mass balance, variability, control, quality, safety, and yield may change with scale | geometric extrapolation or lab yield is called commercial readiness; owner in `14`/`15` |
| Verification–validation matrix | assurance representation | requirements, hypotheses, tests, environments, evidence, and acceptance must trace | test completion is called fitness for use or changing requirements lose traceability |

## Portfolio, partnerships, IP, and transfer

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Innovation portfolio management | portfolio method | projects compete across value, strategic fit, uncertainty, timing, dependencies, and learning | single ROI rank kills options or categories become fixed quotas |
| Discovery-driven planning | uncertainty-management method | new ventures need assumptions, reverse income statements, milestones, and learning | assumptions are not tested or target economics are manipulated to justify project |
| Real-options portfolio | staged investment method | defer, expand, pivot, partner, or abandon choices have material value | “optionality” excuses indefinite weak projects without expiry and evidence |
| Innovation accounting | learning measurement family | early ventures need hypothesis/evidence/progress metrics before mature financials | vanity activity or custom metrics evade outcome/economic accountability |
| Kill / pivot / persevere gate | portfolio decision | evidence should change commitment and path | sunk cost, politics, or moving goalposts prevent termination |
| Innovation partnership — ISO 56003:2019 | guidance framework | partners need fit, selection, alignment, interaction, governance, and exit | partnership label hides incentives, IP, data, authority, and asymmetry |
| Open-innovation challenge | sourcing method | external solvers can address a well-framed problem with clear evaluation/reward | sensitive problem, unclear IP, adverse selection, or sponsor cannot adopt outputs |
| Technology scouting | search/evaluation method | external technologies, suppliers, labs, startups, and evidence may fill capability gaps | demo hype, no search protocol, or scouting lacks decision owner |
| Patent landscape | IP/technology intelligence method | claims, assignees, families, jurisdictions, citations, and white space inform strategy | patent count equals quality/freedom-to-operate or legal conclusions lack counsel |
| IP management — ISO 56005:2020 | guidance framework | innovation strategy must govern creation, protection, sharing, acquisition, and risk | “patent everything” or secrecy blocks collaboration and value |
| Freedom-to-operate analysis | legal/IP risk method | commercialization may infringe enforceable claims in relevant jurisdictions | keyword search or agent provides legal opinion without qualified counsel |
| Technology transfer | commercialization/knowledge-transfer process | research outputs need disclosure, evaluation, protection, licensing/startup/partner pathway | publication, inventor incentives, rights, tacit knowledge, and adoption capacity are ignored |
| Licensing strategy | commercialization method | rights can be field/territory/time/exclusivity/performance structured | exclusivity is granted without milestones, diligence, reversion, and public-interest constraints |
| Corporate venture capital | strategic investment model | minority investments may create learning, options, ecosystem, and returns | strategic rationale excuses poor governance, conflicts, valuation, and follow-on discipline |
| Accelerator / incubator | venture-support program family | selected ventures benefit from time-bounded resources, mentoring, network, and experiments | program theater optimizes cohorts/demo day rather than venture evidence and survival |

## Measurement and assurance

| Candidate | Type and output | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Innovation operation measurement — ISO 56008:2024 | measurement guidance | inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes, and system learning need governed measures | standardized metric set is assumed or measures become targets |
| Evidence/readiness ledger | governance artifact | claims, maturity, experiments, results, uncertainties, owners, and next gates need traceability | optimistic slide deck replaces raw evidence and negative results |
| Innovation funnel metrics | flow sensors | opportunity intake, cycle, attrition, experiments, decisions, and bottlenecks need monitoring | high idea count/low kill rate is assumed better or quality disappears |
| Portfolio balance and exposure | risk measurement | investment by horizon, domain, uncertainty, dependency, and downside needs visibility | target percentages replace strategy and correlations |
| Learning velocity | learning metric family | decision-relevant uncertainty retired per time/cost needs improvement | experiment count or speed rewards trivial tests and weak evidence |
| Independent technical review | assurance gate | high-stakes feasibility/readiness claims need challenge by qualified reviewers | reviewers lack data, independence, domain competence, or decision authority |
| Reproducibility / replication check | research assurance | critical experimental findings must survive repeat execution and variation | exact replication is impossible/irrelevant or negative replication is buried |
| Commercialization readiness review | cross-functional gate | technology, product, manufacturing, IP, regulation, supply, service, market, and economics must align | one readiness score averages fatal gaps or launch date drives approval |

## Composition patterns

### Move an opportunity from signal to investment

`strategy/thesis → strategic intelligence + opportunity evidence → framing/ideas → concept comparison → cheapest decisive experiments → technology/readiness plan → IP/partner/regulatory/economic gates → staged portfolio decision → pilot/demonstration → commercialization readiness`

### Mature a technology

`mission/use requirements → baseline TRL/TMA + gaps/AD2 → maturation hypotheses → DOE/prototypes → integration/manufacturing/scale evidence → independent V&V → representative demonstration → readiness review → transfer/production plan`

### Operate an innovation portfolio

`portfolio roles + constraints → comparable evidence ledger → uncertainty/options/dependencies → staged funding → learning milestones → kill/pivot/persevere gates → balance/exposure review → capability and outcome learning`
